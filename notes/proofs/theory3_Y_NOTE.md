## 1. The layer flow, normalisation, the unit

**The flow.** A layer has the block $S$ ($k-1$ formed units, critical point $\hat y_{k-1}$ of the fit of the target by these units) and the entrant $e$ (kernel at $c^0_e$, offset $d_0=|c_e^0-c^*_{f(e)}|$ from the feature
assigned to it), all other kernels dormant ($\Omega=0$). Units $i\in S\cup\{e\}$ have the state $(x_i,c_i)$, $x_i=\alpha_i/a_{\max}$. By Lemma S1 the units of the network lie on their critical curve exactly:
$Q_i=\beta_i-v_s\cosh(2\alpha_i/a_{\max})$ is constant along the flow for any drive and any motion of the centres, and $Q_i(0)=\varepsilon b_i-v_s$ for a unit started at $\alpha=0$, $\beta=\varepsilon b_i$, i.e.
$\beta_i=\varepsilon b_i+2v_s\sinh^2x_i$ (Lemma S1(i)). Inserting it in $\dot\alpha=G\beta a'g$ ($a'=\operatorname{sech}^2x$) gives the closed system ($b_i=1$)
$$\dot x_i=\tfrac G{a_{\max}}q_\varepsilon(x_i)\,g_i,\quad \dot c_i=\mu_cw_ih_i,\quad q_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon+(2v_s-\varepsilon)\tanh^2x=\frac{\varepsilon+2v_s\sinh^2x}{\cosh^2x},\quad w=W_\varepsilon(x)=\tfrac{a_{\max}}m\tanh x\,(\varepsilon+2v_s\sinh^2x),\tag{1.1}$$
with $g_i=\sum_mA_m\Gamma(c_i-c^*_m)-\sum_jw_j\Gamma(c_i-c_j)$, $h_i=\sum_mA_m\Gamma'(c_i-c^*_m)-\sum_{j\ne i}w_j\Gamma'(c_i-c_j)$ (the sums over the layer's units; the target's features $m=1..K$ all present, fitted or not).
$\varepsilon>0$: the network's own flow; $\varepsilon=0$: the limit object's layer ($\beta=2v_s\sinh^2x$, Lemma S1(i) at $\varepsilon=0$). The vector field is real-analytic in $(x,c,\varepsilon)$ on all of $\mathbb R^{2(k)}\times[0,2v_s)$; its only degeneracy at $\varepsilon=0$ is that
$\{x_e=0\}$ consists of equilibria of the $e$-component ($\dot x_e=0$, $\dot c_e=0$).

**The coefficient is a gradient flow with a positive mobility.** $\dot w=W'(x)\dot x=M_\varepsilon(w)\,g$ with $M_\varepsilon=\tfrac Gm\,q_\varepsilon(q_\varepsilon+4v_s\sinh^2x)$ ($=dW/d\eta$, THEORY_T1 Lemma 2.1), positive for $w\ne0$ ($M_\varepsilon(0)=G\varepsilon^2/m$, $M_0(0)=0$),
and $g=-\partial_wF$, $\dot c=-\mu_c\partial_cF$ (Lemma 1.1 of THEORY_NOTE). So the layer flow is the flow of Prop. A.

**Normalisation.** Amplitudes and coefficients in units of $a_{\min}$: $v_i=\epsilon_iw_i/a_{\min}$ ($\epsilon_i=\operatorname{sgn}A_{f(i)}$), $d_i=c_i-c^*_{f(i)}$, $F'=F/a_{\min}^2$; then $\dot v_i=-M_\varepsilon(a_{\min}v_i)\partial_{v_i}F'$, $\dot d_i=-\mu_ca_{\min}^2\partial_{d_i}F'$. Box data
$\bar v,E_v,E_c,v_{lo},\tilde d$ as in Prop. A; $\hat d=\tilde d+0.05(r-\tilde d)$, margin $\varsigma=0.01$, relaxation set $\hat D=\{|d|\le\hat d,\ v\in[\Gamma(\hat d)-E_v-\varsigma,\ R+E_v+\varsigma]\}$ (convex, $\supset D^*$);
$s_{\max}=R\psi(\hat d)$, $t_{\min}=(\Gamma(\hat d)-E_v-\varsigma)(\tfrac12-\tfrac{\hat d^2}4)\Gamma(\hat d)$, $\lambda_{\rm own}$ the least eigenvalue of $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&s_{\max}\\ s_{\max}&t_{\min}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\nu=\lambda_{\rm own}-H_c$,
$H_{up}=\max(1,\tfrac R2\bar v)+s_{\max}+H_c$. Hypotheses:

* **(H)** $\ell=\Delta-2r\ge\sqrt6$ ($K\ge2$), $\kappa_c=(K-1)\Gamma(\ell)<1$, $r<\sqrt2$, $r>d_0$ (strictly), and **(L)** $v_{lo}>0$, **(C)** $\psi(r)>E_c$ (these give box invariance and the monotone entry);
* **(N-hat)** $\Gamma(\hat d)-E_v-\varsigma>0$ and $\nu>0$. ((N-hat) $\Rightarrow$ (N) of Prop. A: $\lambda_{\rm own}$ decreases with $s$ and increases with $t$, and $\hat d>\tilde d$.)

$\Omega=0$ throughout. $F'$ restricted to $\hat D^{k}$ has $\nu I\preceq\nabla^2F'\preceq H_{up}I$ (own blocks $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&s\\ s&t\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ with $|s|\le s_{\max}$, $t\ge t_{\min}$, $t\le\tfrac R2\bar v$, plus a cross part of spectral norm $\le H_c$: THEORY_NOTE Step 3, THEORY_T1 Lemma 4.2).

## 2. Lemma E0 (the tail of the entrant; uniform in $\varepsilon$)

**Lemma E0.** Let $0<w_\#$, $x_\#=W_0^{-1}(w_\#)$. For every $\varepsilon\in[0,2v_s)$ and $0<w\le w_\#$
$$M_\varepsilon(w)\ \ge\ c_Mw^{4/3},\qquad c_M=\frac{Gm}{a_{\max}^2}\Bigl(\frac{2v_sa_{\max}}{m\cosh^4x_\#}\Bigr)^{2/3}.$$
Consequently, if along a solution of (1.1) $\dot w_e=M_\varepsilon(w_e)g_e$ with $g_e\ge w_\#$ whenever $w_e\le w_\#$ (true in $B_0$ when $w_\#\le a_{\min}v_{lo}/2$, since $g_e\ge a_{\min}v_{lo}-w_e$, THEORY_T1 Lemma 3.1(b)), then $\frac d{dt}w_e^{-1/3}\le-\kappa_0:=-c_Mw_\#/3$ and
$$w_e(t)\le\bigl(w_\#^{-1/3}+\kappa_0(t_\#-t)\bigr)^{-3}\quad(t\le t_\#),\qquad\int_{-\infty}^{t_\#}w_e\,dt\le\frac{w_\#^{2/3}}{2\kappa_0}.\tag{2.1}$$

*Proof.* $q_\varepsilon=(\varepsilon+2v_s\sinh^2x)/\cosh^2x$ and $w=\frac{a_{\max}}m\tanh x(\varepsilon+2v_s\sinh^2x)$ give $q_\varepsilon=\dfrac{mw}{a_{\max}\sinh x\cosh x}$. Hence $M_\varepsilon\ge\frac Gmq_\varepsilon^2=\dfrac{Gmw^2}{a_{\max}^2\sinh^2x\cosh^2x}$.
From $w\ge\frac{2v_sa_{\max}}m\tanh x\sinh^2x=\frac{2v_sa_{\max}}m\frac{\sinh^3x}{\cosh x}$: $\sinh^3x\le\frac{mw\cosh x}{2v_sa_{\max}}$, so $\sinh^2x\cosh^2x=(\sinh^3x)^{2/3}\cosh^2x\le\bigl(\tfrac{mw}{2v_sa_{\max}}\bigr)^{2/3}\cosh^{8/3}x$. Therefore
$M_\varepsilon\ge\frac{Gm}{a_{\max}^2}\bigl(\frac{2v_sa_{\max}}m\bigr)^{2/3}w^{4/3}\cosh^{-8/3}x$. Finally $W_\varepsilon\ge W_0$ pointwise, so $x_\varepsilon(w)\le x_0(w)\le x_\#$ and $\cosh^{-8/3}x\ge\cosh^{-8/3}x_\#=(\cosh^4x_\#)^{-2/3}$. For (2.1):
$\frac d{dt}w^{-1/3}=-\frac13w^{-4/3}\dot w\le-\frac13c_Mg_e\le-\kappa_0$; integrate backwards from $t_\#$ ($w_e(t_\#)=w_\#$) and integrate $(w_\#^{-1/3}+\kappa_0\tau)^{-3}$ over $\tau\in(0,\infty)$. $\square$

Numerically (`theory3y_entry.c_tail`): $M_\varepsilon(w)/(c_Mw^{4/3})\ge28$ on $w\in[10^{-12},w_\#]$, $\varepsilon\in\{0,10^{-12},\dots,10^{-2}\}$, $w_\#\in\{0.05,0.3,0.34,0.9\}$: the constant is not sharp (it drops $4v_s\sinh^2x$ and bounds $\cosh$ by its value at $x_\#$), it is uniform.
The consequence: the dormant state is left **only at the algebraic rate $w_e\sim|t|^{-3}$ as $t\to-\infty$** (the exact leading behaviour is $w\simeq\frac{a^4_{\max}}{4G^3v_s^2m}\,(\text{distance to the wall})^{-3}$, THEORY_T1 Cor. 2.3), which is integrable, and $\int|\dot c_e|\,dt\le\mu_c(\sup|C_e|+R\psi_{\max})\int w_e<\infty$: the entrant's centre has a limit at $-\infty$. Nothing else about the degeneracy at $w_e=0$ is needed below.

## 3. Lemma E1: the closed box contains the dormant state

Let $B_0=\{|d_i|\le r\ \forall i;\ v_i\in[v_{lo},\bar v],\ i\in S;\ v_e\in[0,\bar v]\}$ (the entrant's lower face is now $v_e=0$) and $B_{v_0}=B_0\cap\{v_e\ge v_0\}$.

**Lemma E1** (Prop. A (i),(iii) with the face $v_e=0$ included; $\varepsilon\ge0$). Assume (H). (a) For $\varepsilon>0$ $B_0$ is positively invariant. For $\varepsilon\ge0$ every solution with $v_e(0)\in(0,v_{lo}]$, $(v_i,d_i)(0)\in B_0$ stays in $B_{v_e(0)}\subset B_0$.
(b) In $B_0$: $-\partial_{v_e}F'=\epsilon_eg_e/a_{\min}\ge\Gamma(r)-E_v-v_e\ge v_{lo}-v_e$ (the drive on the entrant is positive and bounded below **uniformly down to $v_e=0$**); hence $\dot v_e>0$ for $0<v_e<v_{lo}$ if $\varepsilon=0$ (for $v_e\ge0$ if $\varepsilon>0$),
$v_e$ crosses each level $\vartheta\in(0,v_{lo}]$ once, transversally, and $v_e(t)\ge\min(v_e(0),v_{lo})$ for all $t$. (c) $|d_i(t)|\le\max(|d_i(0)|,\tilde d)$, and $\frac d{dt}|d_i|\le-\mu_ca_{\min}^2v_i\frac{\Gamma(r)}2(|d_i|-\tilde d)$ when $|d_i|\ge\tilde d$. (d) With (N) also: convergence to the unique critical point $\hat y_k\in D^*$ of the fit of the units $S\cup\{e\}$ (for every $\varepsilon\ge0$ and every positive mobility).

*Proof.* Prop. A Steps 1-2 and THEORY_T1 Lemma 3.1 verbatim: in $B_0$ the distance of $c_i$ to every other unit and unfitted feature is $\ge\ell$, so $|P_i|\le E_v$, $|C_i|\le E_c$; faces: $v_i=\bar v$: $-\partial_vF'\le a_i-\bar v+E_v<0$; $v_i=v_{lo}$ (block): $\ge\Gamma(r)-v_{lo}-E_v\ge0$ (strict for $v_{lo}$ chosen slightly below); $v_e=0$: $-\partial_{v_e}F'\ge\Gamma(r)-E_v>0$ and for $\varepsilon>0$ the mobility is positive, so the face points inward; for $\varepsilon=0$ a solution with $v_e>0$ never reaches $v_e=0$ (uniqueness of solutions, $\{x_e=0\}$ invariant) and the lower face is $v_e(0)$; $|d_i|=r$: $\frac d{dt}|d_i|<0$ by (C). Nagumo. (b),(c) read off. (d) $\dot F'\le0$ with equality in $B_0$ only at critical points of $F'$ (the entrant's $v_e>0$ for $t>0$ if $\varepsilon>0$ since $\dot v_e>0$ at $v_e=0$; and $g_e>0$ excludes the non-critical equilibria with $v_e=0$); LaSalle gives a connected set of critical points in $B_0\cap\{|d|\le\tilde d\}$, unique by (N) (strict convexity on $D^*$, Prop. A Step 4). $\square$

**Remark (what "from the dormant boundary to the box" was).** The old paragraph asked for the orbit to *reach* $B$ from the dormant state. There is nothing to reach: the dormant state $P_0=(\hat y_{k-1};\,v_e=0;\,d_e=c_e^0-c^*_{f(e)})$ is a point of $B_0$ ($|d_e|\le d_0<r$, the block in $D^*_{S}\subset{\rm int}\,B$ because $\tilde d<r$ and $v_{lo}<\Gamma(\tilde d)-E_v$, $R+E_v<\bar v$) and $B_0$ is invariant.
The degeneracy $M_0(0)=0$ only slows the exit, it does not change the sign of the drive on the face. The only thing the degeneracy costs is that the exit takes infinite time, with the explicit tail of Lemma E0.

## 4. Theorem E1: existence, location and convergence of the entering orbit ($\varepsilon=0$)

**Definition.** An *entering orbit* of the layer is a solution $\omega:\mathbb R\to X$ of (1.1) at $\varepsilon=0$ with $v_e(t)>0$ and $\omega(t)\to P_0$ as $t\to-\infty$.

**Theorem E1.** Assume (H), (N). (i) Every entering orbit lies in $B_0$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$; $\dot v_e>0$ while $v_e<v_{lo}$; $|d_e(t)|\le\max(d_0,\tilde d)$ for all $t$; $v_e$ satisfies the tail (2.1) before its crossing of any level $w_\#\le a_{\min}v_{lo}/2$; and $\omega(t)\to\hat y_k$ as $t\to+\infty$.
(ii) Entering orbits exist. More precisely, let $x^{(v_0)}$ be the solution started at $(\hat y_{k-1},v_0,c^0_e)$, $0<v_0\le v_{lo}$, and $x^{(v_0)}(\cdot+t_\#(v_0))$ its time-shift to the first crossing ($t_\#$) of the level $w_\#$. For every $v_0^{(n)}\to0$ a subsequence converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb R$ to an entering orbit $\omega$ with $\omega(0)$ at the level. (Note $t_\#(v_0)\to\infty$.)

*Proof.* (i) $\omega(t)\to P_0$ and $P_0\in B_0$ with all faces other than $v_e=0$ strictly inactive (see the Remark), so there is $t_1$ with $\omega(t)\in B_0$ for $t\le t_1$, and $v_e(t_1)\in(0,v_{lo}]$. By Lemma E1(a) $\omega(t)\in B_{v_e(t_1)}\subset B_0$ for $t\ge t_1$: the whole orbit. $\dot v_e>0$: Lemma E1(b). $|d_e|$: by E1(c), $|d_e|$ is nonincreasing whenever $\ge\tilde d$; if $|d_e(t)|>\max(d_0,\tilde d)$ for some $t$ then $|d_e|\ge|d_e(t)|$ at all earlier times (it is nondecreasing backwards while $\ge\tilde d$), contradicting $|d_e(-\infty)|=|c_e^0-c^*|\le d_0$. Tail: Lemma E0 ($w_\#\le a_{\min}v_{lo}/2$ so $g_e\ge w_\#$ on $w_e\le w_\#$). Convergence at $+\infty$: Lemma E1(d), applied to $\omega(t_1+\cdot)$.
(ii) Fix $v_0^{(n)}\to0$ and write $x_n(t)=x^{(v_0^{(n)})}(t+t_\#^{(n)})$, defined on $[-t_\#^{(n)},\infty)$ with $x_n(t)\in B_0$ (Lemma E1(a)). The vector field is smooth and bounded on the compact set $B_0$ (in the coordinates $(x,c)$, $B_0$ is compact), so $\{x_n\}$ is equi-Lipschitz; Arzelà-Ascoli and a diagonal argument give a subsequence converging uniformly on every $[-N,N]$ to a continuous curve $\omega$ with values in $B_0$, which is a solution (pass to the limit in the integral equation), defined as long as the $x_n$ are, i.e. on all of $\mathbb R$ **provided** $t_\#^{(n)}\to\infty$: if $t_\#^{(n)}\le T$ along a subsequence, the limit of $x_n$ on $[-T',0]$ would be the solution started at $v_e=0$ with $x_e=0$ for all times (continuous dependence on the initial data on bounded time intervals, $\{x_e=0\}$ invariant), contradicting $w_e(x_n(0))=w_\#>0$. So $t_\#\to\infty$ (in fact $t_\#(v_0)\sim v_0^{-1/3}$ by (2.1) and the upper bound $M_0\le C w^{4/3}$, numerically verified in the report).
Properties of $\omega$: $\omega(0)$ is at the level $w_\#$, $v_e(\omega(t))>0$ for all $t$ (a solution with $v_e(0)>0$), $\omega\subset B_0$, $v_e$ nondecreasing (a limit of increasing functions). (1) $v_e(t)\to0$ as $t\to-\infty$: $v_e\downarrow v_\infty\ge0$; if $v_\infty>0$, the $\alpha$-limit set $\mathcal A\subset B_0$ of $\omega$ is non-empty, compact, invariant and contained in $\{v_e=v_\infty\}$; a solution through a point of $\mathcal A$ has $\dot v_e>0$ there (E1(b): $v_\infty\le w_\#<v_{lo}$), contradicting invariance. (2) $c_e(t)\to c_e^0$: $|\dot c_e|\le C_cw_e$ with $C_c=\mu_ca_{\min}(R\psi_{\max}+E_c)$ (physical units: $\dot c_e=\mu_cw_eh_e$, $|h_e|\le a_e\psi(d_e)+|C_e|$), and the tail (2.1) holds for **every** $x_n$ on $[-t_\#^{(n)},0]$ with the same constants, so
$|c_e^{(n)}(t)-c_e^0|\le C_c\int_{-\infty}^t(w_\#^{-1/3}+\kappa_0|\tau|)^{-3}d\tau=\dfrac{C_c}{2\kappa_0(w_\#^{-1/3}+\kappa_0|t|)^2}$, uniformly in $n$; the same bound holds for the limit, which gives $c_e(t)\to c_e^0$ as $t\to-\infty$ (and $d_e\to c_e^0-c^*_{f(e)}$). (3) $\mathcal A=\{P_0\}$: $\mathcal A\subset\{v_e=0,\ c_e=c_e^0\}\cap B_0$ is compact invariant; on this set (1.1) is the gradient flow of the block's own fit $F_S$ (the entrant is dormant, $\dot c_e=0$) with a strict Lyapunov function; in $B_0$ the block fit has the unique critical point $\hat y_{k-1}$ ((N) for the sub-block: the same Hessian argument, fewer units); every solution in $\mathcal A$ is bounded in both directions, so its $\alpha$- and $\omega$-limits are critical points, hence $\hat y_{k-1}$, so $F_S$ is constant along it and it is the equilibrium. Therefore $\omega(t)\to P_0$. $\square$

## 5. Proposition E2: uniqueness of the entering orbit up to a time shift ($\varepsilon=0$)

**Proposition E2.** Under (H), (N), if $\omega_1,\omega_2$ are entering orbits of the same layer, then $\omega_2(t)=\omega_1(t+\tau)$ for some $\tau$. (Hence the sequences of Theorem E1(ii) converge without passing to a subsequence, and E4's limit is unique.)

*Proof.* Write $a=\alpha_e=a_{\max}x_e$ (increasing along an entering orbit while $v_e<v_{lo}$: $\dot a=G\beta(a)a'(a)g_e$ with $g_e\ge a_{\min}v_{lo}/2$), $y$ the block coordinates $(x_i,c_i)_{i\in S}$, $c$ for $c_e$. For $a\le a_\#$ ($v_e\le w_\#$): $c_1a^2\le\varphi:=\dot a\le c_2a^2$ with explicit $c_{1,2}$ ($\beta=2v_s\sinh^2(a/a_{\max})\in[2v_sa^2/a_{\max}^2,\,C a^2]$, $a'\in[\operatorname{sech}^2(a_\#/a_{\max}),1]$).
Each orbit is a graph $(y(a),c(a))$ over $a\in(0,a_\#]$ solving the regular system $y'=X_y/\varphi$, $c'=X_c/\varphi$ ($'=d/da$) with $X_y=-\mathbb M(y)\nabla_yF(y;c,a)$ (positive diagonal mobility $\mathbb M$, smooth) and $X_c=\mu_cw_e(a)h_e$, $|X_c|\le C_3a^3$; $y(a)\to\hat y_{k-1}$, $c(a)\to c^0_e$ as $a\to0$ (E1 definition).
Let $\Delta=y_1-y_2$, $\delta=c_1-c_2$ for two such orbits, evaluated at the same $a$. *(a) Contraction.* For $a\le a_0$ both $y_i(a)$ lie in a small ball around $\hat y_{k-1}$, interior to $\hat D$, so the segment $[y_2,y_1]$ is in the convex set where $\nabla^2_{yy}F\succeq\nu_B>0$ (the block Hessian at $w_e\approx0$; this is (N) for the sub-block, with the weighted norm $\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb M^{-1}}$ for which $\mathbb M\nabla^2F\sim$ is dissipative). (The weight $\mathbb M(y_1(a))^{-1}$ varies along the orbit: its derivative is $O(|X_y(1)|/\varphi_1)=o(1)/a^2$, absorbed in the same way.) Then
$\Delta'=\frac{X_y(1)}{\varphi_1}-\frac{X_y(2)}{\varphi_2}=-\frac1{\varphi_1}\mathbb M(y_1)\bigl[\bar H\Delta+\bar K\delta\bigr]-\frac1{\varphi_1}(\mathbb M(y_1)-\mathbb M(y_2))\nabla_yF(y_2)+X_y(2)\bigl(\frac1{\varphi_1}-\frac1{\varphi_2}\bigr)$
with $\bar H\succeq\nu_B$ the averaged Hessian, $|\bar K|\le C a^3$ (the block's coupling to $c_e$ carries the factor $w_e=O(a^3)$). *(b) The error terms are small in a way that needs no rate.* $\nabla_yF(y_2;c_2,a)$ and $X_y(2)$ are $o(1)$ as $a\to0$ (the orbit tends to the zero of $\nabla_yF$ at $P_0$); $|\varphi_1-\varphi_2|\le C a^2(|\Delta|+|\delta|)$ (Lipschitz dependence of $g_e$), so $|\frac1{\varphi_1}-\frac1{\varphi_2}|\le\frac{C(|\Delta|+|\delta|)}{a^2}$.
Hence for $a\le a_0$ small enough that the $o(1)$ terms are below $\nu_B/(4c_2)$:
$$\frac d{da}\tfrac12\|\Delta\|^2_{\mathbb M^{-1}}\le-\frac{\nu_B}{2c_2a^2}\|\Delta\|^2+\frac{\eta(a)}{a^2}\|\Delta\|\,|\delta|+Ca\|\Delta\||\delta|,\qquad\eta(a)\to0.$$
So with $N(a)=\sup_{s\le a}|\delta(s)|$: $\|\Delta(a)\|\le C'\eta^*(a)N(a)$, $\eta^*(a)=\sup_{s\le a}\eta(s)$ (variation of constants with the kernel $e^{-(\nu_B/2c_2)(1/s-1/a)}$, whose integral against $s^{-2}$ is bounded by $2c_2/\nu_B$).
*(c) The centre.* $\delta'=\frac{X_c(1)}{\varphi_1}-\frac{X_c(2)}{\varphi_2}$, $|\delta'|\le Ca(\|\Delta\|+|\delta|)$ (Lipschitz $h_e$ in $(y,c)$, $|X_c|\le C_3a^3$, $|\frac1{\varphi_1}-\frac1{\varphi_2}|\le C(\|\Delta\|+|\delta|)a^{-2}$). The stress layers show where it fails: $K=2$, $R=2.4$, $d_0=0.4$: at $\Delta=4.9$ (L),(C) hold ($v_{lo}=0.86$, $\psi(r)-E_c=0.035$) but $\nu=-1.29$; at $\Delta=5.2$: $\nu=-0.54$; from $\Delta=5.6$: $\nu=0.027>0$.
The obstruction is the *crude cross-Hessian bound* $H_c$ (the row sum with the triangle inequality, the factor $R+\bar v\le2R/(1-\kappa_c)$), not the layer: the integrated flows converge in every stress layer: in a scratch run (not in the report, which integrates only the layers in the regime) the six layers outside it ($R=2.4$, $\Delta=4.9,5.2$) were integrated at $\varepsilon=0$ from $v_0=10^{-6}$ to $t'=3\cdot10^5$ and end at the target to machine precision (6 of 6); THEORY_NOTE §B shows the inner problem ending at the target for all $\Delta\ge4\sigma$ in 108 runs. A sharper Hessian bound (block spectral norm of the cross part) would move the threshold as in THEORY_NOTE's remark on $\Delta_{\rm entry}$ ($4.56$ instead of $4.66$ for $K=2$).
For $K=1$: $\tilde d=0$, $D^*=\{d=0,\ v\in[1,R]\}$ is a face; the flow enters every neighbourhood but never $D^*$; $\hat D$ is the right set. For $n>1$: replace $d$ by Euclidean offsets and the interval radius by a ball; the box faces, the tail lemma and the compactness argument are dimension-free; the Hessian constants (the own block is then $(1+n)\times(1+n)$ and the cross part has more terms) are not re-derived here.
