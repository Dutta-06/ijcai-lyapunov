# REVIEW_roundCD: referee report on the dual-order regime study (C) and the cluster-dependence study (D)

Archived verbatim from the referee's hand-back (2026-11-09). The referee was read-only for the repository; its scratch code is outside the repository.

REFEREE REPORT C+D (read-only; scratch code in referee_CD_work/: dr1-5.py, cd1-2.py)
Overall: no blocker. Timestamps, seed separation, freeze hashes and every headline number reproduce. The problems are wording and a few scoring and design weaknesses. Both reports are honest about most of them, but some generated sentences and the plan's section-9 template overstate.

== STUDY 1: dual-order regime (workstream C) ==
F1 (major, wording) "The lead is removed by a backward step" (plan section 9 template) is false. P1 = +2.1 [1.5, 2.8] and Q1 = +3.4 [2.7, 4.2] stay wins. The lead shrinks from +7.8 (P2) and +8.0 (Q2); it is not removed. Conclusion 6 ("modest once a backward step is allowed") is the correct text. Fix: never use "removed"; say "reduced to about 2-3 points".
F2 (major, wording) "The dual order matters: no" is driven only by the P4/Q4 loss against sliding Frank-Wolfe (sFW). That is a different algorithm, not an order comparison. Fix: separate two statements. (i) Order effect, everything else equal: small win in 1-D close pairs and crowds, loss with unequal free widths (W2, W3), and in 2-D a win at 1.2-1.6 widths but a loss at 3.0. (ii) sFW is better than the ISS in 1-D pairs and crowds.
F3 (major, wording) The generated lines "W4/W5: dual beats sFW (+16.6, +38.9)" are misspecification artefacts: the plan says the fixed-width sFW is not counted in W. Do not quote W4 to W8. P10 (+33.9) and Q10 (+19.7) compare against an 8%-exact entrant-significance rule for greedy; do not quote them. P9 and Q9 (the order with no stopping rule, +19.9, +18.6) are legitimate as order-only numbers, but state that they use oracle K.
F4 (major, equivalence scope) The 797/800 result covers 1-D pairs only (K=2, amplitude ratios 1 and 10, snr 8 and 12, r_ex 1.5), at separations 3.5 and 5.0. It compares final BIC blocks (centres within 0.05 widths), not entry sequences. Part T shows the entry sequences differ in 0-4 of 12 phases at 3.5 widths and agree only from about 5 widths. The 3 disagreements are all ratio-10 cells, where greedy keeps an extra noise unit (rerun: dual 2 units, greedy 3). The 2-D arm shows greedy ahead by -39 [-47, -31.5] at 3.0 widths, and 2-D at 3.5 widths or more was never run. So "equivalent from 3.5 widths" must not be generalised to images.
F5 (minor) Part T: the plan's expectations missed in the report's own item 7. Noise-free pairs only. The "continuum entry threshold 4.7-5.8 widths" sentence is hard-coded in the script and I did not verify it.
F6 (minor, design) The backward step is applied only to blocks of at most K_true+1 units (apply_rules uses len(c_true)), so K leaks into the bw arms. Rerun on 180 P, 120 W and 80 Q held-out scenes without the cap: P identical; W greedy+bw 78 to 82 of 120, dual+bw 81 to 82; Q greedy+bw 8 to 10 of 80. The cap slightly handicaps greedy+bw, so P1, Q1 and W1 are slightly inflated (by about 0 to 3 points) and P3 and Q3 are not affected. Cheapest fix: say so in the report, or rerun W/Q with PRUNE_EXTRA=3.
F7 (major, mechanism) "Where the order matters it is memory" and "hurts through stale duals" are hypotheses. Evidence is one planted scene (seed 100002, from the exploratory range) plus an inspection of the first merged 2-D scenes that the text itself calls "not scripted". There is no ablation, such as a leaky or reset accumulator. The post hoc SMLM data do not show the benefit: dual minus greedy recall is negative in 15 of 26 set/band rows and positive only in Bundled_High_Density at under 200 nm (+0.033). Fix: write "consistent with" and drop the causal verb.
F8 (minor) sFW fairness. The ladder (f from 0.5 to 0.003, 8 values) and min_sig=3 are fixed and were not tuned against the dual. sFW has signed weights and shrunken, non-refitted amplitudes, which hurts rather than helps it under BIC. sFW stays ahead without any stopping rule: first block with exactly K units, 79.0 vs dual 73.5 in P (-5.5 [-7.3, -3.8]) and 55.8 vs 47.5 in Q; "some block exact" is 83.8 vs 73.5 in P. So the sFW lead is the estimator, not the penalty ladder. The ladder does give sFW more candidates in the "path" comparison, so do not use "path" as a fair comparison. Equal tuning of dual and greedy holds: the same r_ex 1.5, box, penalties and kappa. r_ex was set for the dual and not re-tuned for greedy (untested).
F9 (minor) The 2-D engine is not exactly "order only": greedy normalises the correlation by a local weighted norm and the dual does not, and the dual enters tied candidates together while greedy takes one. The 1-D engine differs only in order (checked in path()). The 2-D mid loss is dominated by the dual splitting into 3 units (42-58 of 100 at 3.0 widths). Greedy followed by a backward step was not run in 2-D.
F10 (minor, provenance) The plan carries "Recorded 15:13:36Z". It was first committed in 8e522d9 (15:12:22) with a placeholder timestamp, then fixed in 5999e61 (15:13:45). The development raw finished at 15:54:25, after the plan; the plan quotes only the grid C part, which ran first, and I reproduced those numbers from the dev raw. The held-out raw has no start timestamp (file written 16:22:57, committed 16:24:34); only its end is known.

PAPER WORDING (study 1)
"In one-dimensional simulations in which only the order of entry differs (same dictionary, resolution limit, block fit and BIC rule), accumulating the correlation (ISS) recovers the exact model order more often than the largest-instantaneous-correlation order for close pairs (1.2-1.6 widths: +7.8 points [6.7, 9.1], 2000 held-out scenes) and crowded windows (+8.0 [6.9, 9.3], 2400 scenes). Adding a backward step to the greedy order reduces the margin to +2.1 [1.4, 2.8] and +3.4 [2.7, 4.2]. For four features of unequal, freely fitted widths, the greedy order with a backward step is ahead (-18.0 [-20.0, -15.9]). The sliding Frank-Wolfe solver of the BLASSO outperforms the ISS in the first two regimes (-16.2 and -8.2). For pairs separated by 3.5 widths or more (one dimension, two features) the two orders returned the same block in 797 of 800 scenes. In a two-dimensional pair experiment the ISS resolved more pairs at 1.2-1.6 widths (+9.8 points) and fewer at 2.5-3 widths (-19.5). We therefore do not claim that the order of entry is a source of accuracy; it is the order computed by the training dynamics. We suggest, but did not test, that early correlation memory explains the close-pair advantage."

== STUDY 2: cluster dependence ==
G1 (major, primary scoring void on 3 of 8 patches) The offset-corrected truth fails at ngc5904 (640,240): F 0.037 vs 0.374 as is. It also fails at ngc6254 (1900,80): 0.020 vs 0.260, and at ngc6934 (370,1230): 0.014 vs 0.608. These are exactly the three patches whose fitted |offset| is at least 0.46 px (the five working patches have at most 0.36). The plan's reason for correcting ("offsets of 1.0-1.1 px make as-is score near zero") is falsified: as-is F is 0.26-0.61 there, so the fitted offsets are wrong, not the truth misaligned. The report shows this honestly in section 3.3, but the tally uses these patches. Consequences, recomputed with my own stratified block bootstrap (30 seeds for the knife-edge case):
- M10 (ngc6254) has a single patch, so under the primary scoring its verdict carries no information (both F around 0.02-0.03).
- The reported "O2 tie" at M10 is a knife-edge. My bootstrap gives [-0.026, -0.0004], i.e. a loss. Over 30 seeds, 13 give a loss.
- Excluding the 3 failed patches, the primary tally is O2 repaired 3 won / 2 tied / 0 lost over 5 clusters (M5 +0.075, NGC 6535 +0.123, NGC 6934 +0.090). O2f frozen is 3 / 1 / 1 (M71 is the loss, -0.060 [-0.123, -0.007]).
- Truth as is, all 8 patches: O2 3 / 3 / 0 and O2f 3 / 2 / 1, where the frozen loss is M10 (-0.053 [-0.093, -0.013]) and M71 becomes a tie. Excluding the failed patches: 3 / 2 / 0 for both.
So the "3 won / no loss" statement for the repaired ISS stands under every variant, but the number of clusters carrying information is 5, not 6. Whether the frozen ISS loses at M71 (primary) or at M10 (as is) depends on the scoring. Recommendation: report both scorings side by side. Treat the 3 patches as "registration failed, void" (a post hoc exclusion; the plan had no registration gate). Make the as-is scoring the headline robustness check. The offset help is real where the fit works (ngc5466 F 0.281 to 0.455).
G2 (minor-to-major) Amendment A (bright-star limit 14.5 to 13.0, margin 10 to 6 px) was chosen after the dry run so that the first six clusters each keep a patch. The grid of (limit, margin) values tried is not stored, so I cannot audit it. Selection by the rank order of survey windows (M5, NGC 6934, M10, NGC 6535, M71, NGC 5466; M13 is seventh) is geometry only. The direction of bias is not toward easy fields: the amendment admits patches with F606W 13-14.5 stars, and bright stars are where the ISS splits. Three of the six clusters are sparse (0.03-0.04 per px), where ties are expected, and three have PSF status 3 (extrapolated PSF). The 3 failed registrations coincide with patches containing the brightest stars (13.1-13.6 mag); I did not show causation. None of the 6 clusters appears in any earlier scored file (npz key search; only survey and dry-run geometry). Say: "clusters selected by a geometry rule that was amended once after a dry run and before any ISS or baseline catalogue existed".

G5 (major, over-reading) The diagnosis uses n = 5 clusters and 16 covariates, and the covariates are constant within a frame, so n = 19 patches is pseudo-replication. At cluster-mean level (my reproduction): stamp_ell rmse 0.105 vs null 0.130, signs 4 of 5, Spearman -0.70 (n = 5, not significant). The best covariate is rsd_iso (0.050), which is an output of the same ISS fit. A permutation of the cluster gaps (120 permutations) gives p = 14/120 = 0.12 that some covariate among 16 does as well as the observed best for the iso gap. For the rule-row gap nothing beats the null (best/null ratio 0.947). M92 alone drives the iso relationship. The report's erratum admits sky and noise also beat the null. Allowed: "stamp roundness is a candidate explanation, one hypothesis among several, from 5 clusters". Not allowed: "predicts" or "explains".
G6 (major, wording of the 11-cluster tally) 6 won / 3 tied / 2 lost (frozen rule row) mixes: M2, the tuning cluster of the configuration; M53 and M92, whose rule row (the kernel rule) was designed after their iso-row losses; M3 and M15 as spent; and the 6 held-out clusters. For the originally frozen iso row the spent clusters are 2 won / 1 tied / 2 lost, and the held-out iso row is 3 / 2 / 1 (primary) or 3 / 1 / 2 (as is). The 11 clusters are not a random sample. The repaired 7 / 4 / 0 is partly post hoc (rho* chosen on the 5 spent clusters).
