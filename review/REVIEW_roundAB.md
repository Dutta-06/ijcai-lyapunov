# REVIEW_roundAB: referee report on the compute-cost study (speed2) and the second real-data pore study (real2)

Archived verbatim from the referee's hand-back (2026-11-09). The referee was read-only for the repository; its scratch code is outside the repository.

REFEREE REPORT A+B (speed2 and real2). Read-only on the repo. Scratch code and logs: referee_AB_work/ (eq.py, eq2.py, guard*.py, timing.py, astro.py, t1.py, d2.py, d2b.py, b.py).

BOTTOM LINE
- Study 1 (speed2): the SMLM equivalence, the timing numbers and the Picasso baseline all reproduce. The astronomy "variant" is described mostly honestly. There are 5 wording or evidence gaps and no blocker.
- Study 2 (real2): the gates, amendments and hash are clean and the descriptive numbers recompute. The report's silence on one fact is a major issue: on both datasets, Picasso at defaults (R0) beats tuned Picasso (R2E) on the test frames. That undermines every "tuned vs tuned" statement, and the claim "no ISS advantage demonstrated" is only half the fair reading.

FINDINGS

STUDY 1 (speed2)
1. [major, wording] "One to two orders of magnitude" is true only for the fast engine with a single fixed threshold. From speed2_raw.npz: new/Picasso is 5-98x per set (pooled 38x, my bootstrap [30,47]). New without the early exit is 43-175x per set, pooled 106x. The original engine is 200-840x, pooled 465x (2.7 orders). The real2 cost table (guarded original) gives 62-240x. speed2.md prints no prose sentence on "orders of magnitude", although plan section 4 promised one. The no-early-exit/Picasso pooled ratio (106) is never printed. Fix: add one sentence with these three bands.
2. [major, wording] The headline "12.2x" mixes two effects. Per set it is 8.5 / 36 / 43 / 14, and the exact algebra alone is only 3.6-4.8x. The rest is the early exit, which exists only for a single 'snr' rule. N1.HD has interval [7.7,9.4], which fails the plan's own "at least 10x only if the lower end is >= 10" rule. The pooled 12.2 has no interval in the report; mine is [10.95, 13.9]. TT (E-tuned, single threshold) gives 8.9-34.9x. Fix: state "9-43x per set, 12x pooled, 3.6-4.8x of it from exact algebra, the rest from the early exit, which needs one fixed threshold".
3. [major, evidence] The limit-cycle guard is never exercised on real data. The SMLM engines run with cycle_guard=False, as the originals do. On the astronomy patches `barred` is empty for both engines, even on the complete path (my run, patch (110,630), 1321 entries: barred old [] and new []). So "barred set identical" in the report is vacuous. Guard equivalence rests on stubbed block fits. My own planted-cycle worlds (3 seeds x r_ex 0 / 1.2, with my own stub): events and barred lists identical in all 6. But with the real fit after the cycle, 2 of 6 cases differ in blocks. In one case entry times differ by 2.5e-6 relative, above the plan's 1e-6. In the other, block weights differ by a few percent on a flat valley (chi2 equal to 1e-8, nfev 190 vs 200). Fix: say "guard logic equivalent on planted cycles; never triggered on real data".
4. [minor, honesty] Astronomy variant. Complete-path timing reproduces: 182 s vs 18.5 s (9.8x), and AX gives 9.2x on patch (210,630), single runs without repeats. F recomputed from the raw blk arrays (BIC 0.5517/0.5447, kappa 3 0.5545/0.5476, kappa 8 0.4638/0.4631): correct. Two points the text omits. (a) All 9 new-minus-old F differences are negative (-0.0001 to -0.0070). Two of 9 intervals (BIC and kappa 3) exclude 0. The tight-tolerance original moves F in both directions (-0.0062 to +0.0047). So "within the perturbation scale" is true in magnitude, not in sign pattern. (b) Entry counts differ between engines on patch (210,630): 1424 old vs 1420 new, in AX. Only (110,630) is quoted in the report (1321 = 1321). Detection counts at kappa 0 differ (1035 vs 1034; my run). Fix: say "mean F lower by <= 0.007 at every threshold; 2 of 9 intervals exclude 0".
5. [minor] Picasso baseline. It is fair. I installed Picasso 0.11.3 (scratch venv, deleted) and re-ran the repo's worker on 24 dev frames x 3 repeats, with warm-up. N1.LD: 1.58 ms vs 1.57 ms reported. N2.HD: 2.50 ms vs 2.82 ms. Localization counts identical. Same CPU-time metric, same frames. Small caveats: Picasso is timed in its own process, not interleaved with the ISS (plan section 6 and the ENV paragraph say "interleaved"; only old/new are). The warm-up uses frames[:8] of the same set, while the plan says "other frames". Both are harmless. A min-of-3 on a 2 ms job is less load-sensitive than on a 1 s job, but a quiet-machine re-time of the ISS (load < 0.5) agrees with the report: old 0.3195 vs 0.316 s; new/old ratios of 22.8 (N1.LD) and 11.0 (N2.HD) fall within the report's frame-to-frame spread. Picasso is numba-compiled and the ISS is numpy, so the ratio mixes implementation and algorithm.
6. [minor] The scaling exponents rest on 5 sizes and 2-3 frames per size. New 1.31 [0.88,1.76] against old 1.92 [1.60,2.27] overlap. Safe wording: "the fast engine's cost grows about linearly in K, and per entry it is flat (7-8 ms) on the astronomy patch while the original grows from 13 to 41 ms".
7. [pass] Pre-registration timing. The plan is dated "14:23 UTC" and the commit acdb816 is 14:23:53. The script and the addendum (section 5, "no result yet") are in 10dacfd at 14:52:55. The first raw is in 59d225c at 15:12. imaging_fast.py and exp_speed2.py are unchanged after 14:52. Section 6, with the post-hoc parts, is committed with the report at 16:24 and is labelled POST HOC. Disclosure: the plan itself says the thresholds were set after exploratory runs. The commit clock is the only evidence of order.

STUDY 2 (real2)
8. [major, omission] E_s tuning on the dev frames does not transfer. Test E_s, R0 (defaults) vs R2E (tuned): D1 31.1 vs 26.4 (R2E-R0 = -4.7 [-5.5,-4.0]). D2 34.3 vs 10.3 (-23.9 [-30.6,-17.7]). Dev ranking on D2 was R2E 19.4 > R0 12.1 > first-study ISS 11.7; the test ranking is exactly reversed (ISS 53.8 > R0 34.3 > R2E 10.3). The report prints these rows but never says so. Hence "ISS4 - R2E = -10.4" compares two points that are both worse than the untuned baseline. Fix: add this sentence to the report and the paper.
9. [major, balance] The continuity row cuts FOR the method and is under-reported. I recomputed pooled D2 with my own ring fit (scorer geometry from their code): ISS_OLD - R2E = +43.5 [35.6,47.7] (matches), but also ISS_OLD - R0 = +19.6 [11.6,24.7], which the report does not print. The first study had ISS +20.1 [14.3,25.8] on D2. The sign of the D2 contrast therefore flips with the choice of frozen ISS point (4-dial -10.4, 2-dial +43.5). The 2-dial point was frozen in the earlier study (before these frames), but it is outside the rule and on a void pooled set, so it must not be sold as an advantage either.
10. [major, tuning symmetry] The count 721/598 vs 11309/7813 mostly reflects structure: Picasso scans 108 structural settings, the ISS only 8 configurations. In-sample the ISS is at parity (dev E_s 50.30 vs 50.00 on D1; 18.68 vs 19.38 on D2), so more ISS search could buy only noise on dev. What is NOT repaired: the plan's motivation was a grid-edge optimum. D1 is repaired (0 of 8 at kappa 50), but D2 still has 6 of 8 configurations at kappa 50 (disclosed in the last section, not in the summary). The frozen D2 point (hp3, fw 1.5) keeps 1347 localizations (ring N = 86) against 3522 for R2E. Its kappa curve is flat at E ~ -0.1 for kappa 4-30, so the amplitude floor fw x w_ref is what fails to transfer. In Part B (Amendment 2, which widens only Picasso to two structures) the ISS-B D1 optimum sits at the lower edge of the kappa grid (kappa 4). Its dev J_B (0.380) is below untuned Picasso (0.385) and R2B (0.388). So the tuning is not asymmetric in effort, but the ISS grid is truncated where Part B wants it, and this understates the ISS in Part B D1 (-4.3 [-4.9,-3.8]). The D1 result also has higher recall (+4.9) and close-pair recall (+2.8) but more unconfirmed detections (17.0 vs 11.3 per frame); the table's "Picasso better" label hides this.
11. [pass] Gates applied as written. T1: R1 deviation 28.6 nm > 20 in one block (the plan limit). The plan itself (section 0 ii) disclosed the large D1 shift before declaration. T2a: R2 p90 30.1 / 35.0 / 34.4 nm for R0 / R2E / ISS4 against the limit of 30 (R0 only 0.1 over; R2E and ISS4 clearly over). T2b passes the gate but its ring fit is degenerate (N = 0 for both rows, Delta = 0.0 [0,0]). Therefore the pooled -10.4 is driven by void T2a (T2a alone: -16.7 [-28.2,-10.2]). The plan does not say that a pooled dataset with one void member is void; the code does (`overall_A`: any void member makes the dataset void). Minor ambiguity; the result is the same, since T2b alone is uninformative.
12. [pass] Timestamps. Plan 14:32:38; Amendment 1 (14:38:57) states that ref and reg had run but no localizer, calibration or tuning. The registration-gate code is in the 8e522d9 WIP commit at 15:12 and has not changed since (diff checked). Amendment 2 at 15:27:11. The 15:12 raw (59d225c) contains only ref / reg / cal / devpath keys and no dev|*|B keys. The module is committed in d0cbccb at 15:38. Freeze hash: I recomputed sha256 over ref|, reg|, cal|, dev| keys of the stored raw (excluding cal|*B) = 2060a54d...659d, equal to the printed one (time 16:17:25; test 16:17:37-16:50:44). Caveat: the code that produced the first ref/reg outputs (14:32-14:38) is not in any commit before 15:12.
13. [minor, wording] The D1 "equivalent" (+0.5 [-0.4,1.4]) is VOID and POST-AMENDMENT, and the D2 "Picasso better" and Part B "ISS better" labels sit in the main table next to "VOID". Part B D2 is +0.9 [0.0,1.8] (exact lower limit 0.0005, i.e. 0.05 points), inside the +-3 margin, void. A reader scanning the "reading" column will mis-take these as verdicts.
14. [minor, metric] The metric E_s with J_s = N/(N+floor+miss) is dominated by purity (J ~ 0.03-0.40 here). Part B treats real emitters absent from the authors' filtered table as false positives, which penalises the more sensitive localizer. Plan section 5 states this; the report's weaknesses section only points to it.

Wording the paper may use
- Speed (study 1): "A reorganised evaluation of the same fit (Gram-matrix trust-region steps, batched separable kernels, an incremental model and an exact early exit at a fixed significance threshold) returns the same detections on SMLM frames (identical counts on 380 development frames; positions within 2e-10 nm). It cuts the CPU time per frame by 12x pooled (9-43x per set; 3.6-4.8x from the arithmetic alone). The ISS remains about 40x (5-100x per set) slower than Picasso at one fixed threshold, about 100x if the whole path is kept, and about 500x for the original implementation. On the astronomy patches the fast evaluation is a declared variant: about 9-10x faster on complete paths, not event-for-event identical (neither is the original under a ten-fold tighter tolerance), with F lower by at most 0.007 at all nine thresholds." (Add: "development data only; shared 4-core machine; single run per patch.")
- Real data (study 2): "In a pre-registered second test on fresh frames, the registered rule was not concluded: the D1 field failed the registration gate (one block, 28.6 nm vs 20) and the pooled D2 failed the row gate. Descriptively, with all caveats: on D1 the tuned ISS and tuned Picasso were tied (+0.5 E points, [-0.4,1.4]). On D2 the tuned ISS scored below tuned Picasso (-10.4, [-19,-4]), but in both datasets tuning on the development frames did not transfer: untuned Picasso beat tuned Picasso by 4.7 and 23.9 points. We therefore claim no real-data advantage of the ISS, and we do not claim a disadvantage either, since the comparison on D2 is dominated by the failure of the tuning to transfer. On merged-frame (K=3) overlap tests the ISS recovered more bright events (+4.9 and +2.9 points) and more close-pair members (+2.8 and +1.5) but also more unconfirmed detections (ISS-B and the D1 point sit at the lower edge of the kappa grid), giving J_B -4.3 (D1) and +0.9 (D2)." Do not quote +43.5 or +19.6 as evidence of an advantage (outside the rule, void set); if mentioned, label them "post hoc, frozen earlier point, void set".
