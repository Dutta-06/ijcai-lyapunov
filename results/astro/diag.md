## 1. The stamp against the stars (label-using, post hoc)

Stars: truth sources with F606W < 20, more than 12 px from the patch edge, with no other truth source within 4.5 px that is brighter than m + 1.5. Each star: 25 x 25 cut-out around its nearest pixel, minus the median beyond 8.5 px, moved by a cubic spline so that its flux centroid (radius 3 px, 6 iterations) is the middle pixel, cropped to 19 x 19, divided by its sum within 6 px; the stack is the pixelwise MEDIAN. The stamp goes through the same centring and normalisation. The synthetic frame is the exact-stamp world of the patch (section 2: the same stars, a PSF that IS the stamp), so its stack shows what centring does to such a star. **Real minus synthetic** is the difference of the two relative differences of the central pixel (percentage points); "real / synthetic - 1" is the ratio of the central pixels of the two stacks minus 1 (the figure the referee quoted); the last column is the median over the stars of the ratio of the star's two central pixels (real over synthetic) minus 1, a paired estimator. FWHM: `astro_second.psf_fwhm` (px; the referee measured it in rings of 0.5 px, so his figures differ). Intervals: 95 % percentile intervals of a bootstrap over the stars (2000 resamples, the same stars for the real and the synthetic stack, stratified by patch when pooled; a pooled stack is compared with the stamps of its patches averaged with the numbers of stars as weights). A few stars (a companion just beyond 4.5 px, or a sky level over-estimated by the crowd in the ring) have extreme central pixels in both frames; the median stack is robust to them, but the bootstrap interval of a median over a few tens of stars is wide and skewed by them, and the paired column is the sharper statement of the difference between the real and the synthetic stars.

Sets: m2 = M2 (NGC 7089), SDSS run 2583 camcol 2 field 136; m53 = M53 (NGC 5024), SDSS run 5390 camcol 6 field 162; m92 = M92 (NGC 6341), SDSS run 4682 camcol 6 field 208; m2r1 = M2 repeat 1 (NGC 7089), SDSS run 4187 camcol 2 field 105, 2003-10-18, FWHM 2.28 px; m2r2 = M2 repeat 2 (NGC 7089), SDSS run 7778 camcol 6 field 72, 2008-10-30, FWHM 3.05 px; m2r3 = M2 repeat 3 (NGC 7089), SDSS run 3388 camcol 2 field 75, 2002-10-11, FWHM 3.52 px.

## 2. Exact-stamp synthetic worlds

The world of a patch: every truth source of F606W < 30 in the box grown by 20 px, rendered with the patch's own 51 x 51 stamp at its sub-pixel position (cubic spline), flux from the HUGS magnitude by `astro_common.mag_to_counts`, the pipeline sky of the patch, Poisson noise at the patch gain, Gaussian noise of the dark variance; one noise realisation per patch (`default_rng([1108, crc32(set), x0, y0])`). Scored against the injected truth (the HUGS sources of the box) by `astro_common.score`: F of completeness and purity at mag < 22. The ISS at the frozen configuration (sigma_b 3, r_ex 1.2, kappa 2) with the isotropic kernel (**iso**) and with the kernel that the stamp rule chooses (**adequate**; where the rule keeps the isotropic kernel the two are one program on one input and the adequate column shows the isotropic result), crowdsource at its default (**crowdsource**). The real columns are the frozen results of the same patches (`m2_raw.npz`, `psf_raw.npz`, `repeat_raw.npz`). Intervals: 95 % percentile intervals of the block bootstrap (20 x 20 px blocks, 2000 resamples), conditional on the patch and on the one noise realisation of the world. Nothing was tuned on a world.

### Defective runs: paths that reached the entry cap

A path that reaches the entry cap (2500) has stalled: `imaging.sliding_path` can enter and exit one lattice point again and again at the same entry time until the cap (a limit cycle reported after these runs were declared), so that such a run stops after a few hundred distinct entries whatever its stopping rule says. It is a **defective run, not a censored valid one**: every row computed from it is marked STALLED, no table counts it as valid, and the pooled tables and differences leave it out and name it. `src/core/imaging.py` is hash-frozen and was not touched; the rows stay in the raw with their record (`bic`).

Synthetic worlds: 5 of the 30 ISS path runs reached the cap and are defective: m53 (660, 420) ISS, isotropic kernel, a priori default (kappa 5); m92 (1430, 550) ISS, chosen kernel, a priori default (kappa 5); m2r1 (316, 224) ISS, isotropic kernel, a priori default (kappa 5); m2r3 (370, 441) ISS, isotropic kernel, tuned (kappa 2); m2r3 (110, 501) ISS, isotropic kernel, a priori default (kappa 5).
Real data (the frozen results of the same patches): 5 of the 30 ISS path runs copied reached the cap and are defective: m92 (1100, 220) ISS, isotropic kernel, a priori default (kappa 5); m92 (1430, 550) ISS, isotropic kernel, a priori default (kappa 5); m2r2 (133, 281) ISS, isotropic kernel, a priori default (kappa 5); m2r3 (370, 441) ISS, isotropic kernel, a priori default (kappa 5); m2r3 (110, 501) ISS, isotropic kernel, a priori default (kappa 5).

## 3. Splitting and flux bias of bright isolated stars (label-using, post hoc)

A bright isolated star: a truth source in the scored area with F606W < 20.5 and no other truth source within 3 px that is brighter than m_star + 2.5. Its units: the detections of positive flux within 2 px of its truth position; significant: at least 10 % of the star's flux. **Split**: at least 2 significant units. **No unit**: none. The flux ratio is the units' summed flux over the star's. The bright pairs: one-to-one, position-only matching (0.7 px) of the detections in the scored area to the scored truth, truth F606W < 19.5; the median detection-minus-truth magnitude (dmag) and position (dx, dy, px). Intervals: 95 % percentile intervals of a bootstrap over the stars (pairs) resampled within each patch. "Real, all patches": every test patch of the set; "real, the patches of the worlds": the patches that have a synthetic world (the same stars); "synthetic": the worlds. A catalogue of the adequate kernel on a patch where the rule keeps the isotropic kernel is the isotropic catalogue (a set whose patches all keep it has no adequate rows); the rows of the frozen crowdsource post-filter are printed only where they differ from those of the default. The catalogue of an ISS path that stalled (reached the entry cap: a defective run, section 2) is left out of the pool and the last column names the patch; a row counts the patches whose path did not stall, so the real and the synthetic row of a catalogue rest on the same patches only where nothing was left out (the numbers of stars show it).

## Limitations

* One noise realisation per world; the intervals are bootstrap intervals over 20 x 20 px blocks (stars) of that realisation and of the patch, conditional on both. No world was repeated, so a difference that is small against the intervals is not resolved, and the realisation-to-realisation scatter of a world is not measured here (the referee's frames of four of these worlds, `review/REVIEW_roundIS1b_A.md`, are another realisation of them; the F of the same method on the same patch differs between the two realisations).
* The worlds contain only what is put into them. The stamp is exact (no PSF variation over the patch, none beyond 25 px; the stamp is the psField reconstruction at the patch centre, clipped), the noise is exactly Poisson plus Gaussian at the patch gain and dark variance (the real noise has correlations, cosmic rays, flat-field and sky errors), the truth is the HUGS list (sources not in it, and the stars saturated in F606W, are absent from both the world and the scoring), positions and magnitudes are exact (no proper motion or astrometric offset: the image-based truth-to-frame shifts of `second_cluster_spec.md` are not applied; no colour term; the frame's calibration constant differs from the benchmark's, `repeat_spec.md`, which the real data carry and the worlds do not), and nothing saturates: a bright truth star has an unclipped profile in the world and a clipped one in the real frame (maximum DN in the table of the frames), and the world lacks the unresolved light and the sources the HUGS list does not hold (its median DN is lower than the frame's).
* The ISS in a world is given the stamp and fits its own kernel to it as in the real runs (`imaging.fit_psf_parts` or the adequacy rule), so a world tests the kernel misfit; it cannot test a stamp that differs from the stars. The baselines get the exact stamp as in the real runs.
* The stamp stack uses stars brighter than F606W = 20 (a few tens per patch, the table gives the number): it measures the PSF core at high signal to noise, not its wings or its dependence on magnitude or colour, and the interval of a patch is wide. Centring on a noisy centroid and the median stack are estimators with their own bias; the synthetic stack cancels it only to the extent that a rendered star and a real one have the same signal to noise and the same neighbours (the same truth stars are used for both).
* The definitions of section 3 (20.5 mag, 3 px, 2.5 mag, 10 % of the flux, 2 px, 19.5 mag, 0.7 px) are the referee's and were not tuned; they use the truth. A split says that more than one significant unit lies within 2 px of a bright star; it does not say that the units are wrong (a star with a lumpy core may be legitimately fitted by two units): the flux ratio and the magnitude offsets measure the consequence. Pairs are found by position alone, so a split star contributes the unit nearest to it.
* A path that reaches the entry cap has stalled (section 2): its rows are defective, marked STALLED, left out of every pool and named; the paths of the frozen results that stalled are among the real columns, the cause is in `imaging.sliding_path` (hash-frozen, untouched here), and a guarded path is a separate, labelled rerun.
* These controls do not identify a cause of the real M92 or M53 results beyond what the worlds reproduce; a quantity that a world does not reproduce is not thereby explained.
