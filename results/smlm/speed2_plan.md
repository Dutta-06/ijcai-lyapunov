# Pre-registration: the compute cost of the sliding ISS fit, original against a faster evaluation of the same algorithm, against Picasso

**Declared: 2026-11-09 14:23 UTC**, before the final timing run and before any equivalence run of `experiments/exp_speed2.py` (which is written after this file). Everything below uses DEVELOPMENT data only (SMLM development frames, astronomy development patches); no test frame or test patch is read. The exploratory runs made while developing the code (profiles of single frames; an A/B comparison of the two engines on 30 development frames of the four sets, in which the detections agreed in number and to at most 0.09 nm, and on an astronomy path of 250 entries, whose events were identical) were used to debug and are disclosed here; they are not results and are not reported as such. The thresholds of section 3 were set after them and are therefore not independent of them; the equivalence runs below use many more frames. Code under test: `src/core/imaging_fast.py` (new), `experiments/smlm_fast.py` (new), `experiments/astro_fast.py` (new); the originals `src/core/imaging.py` and `src/core/imaging_guard.py` are untouched.

## 1. Question

The paper says the ISS frame fit is one to four orders of magnitude slower than Picasso. (Q1) How much of that is the algorithm and how much is its evaluation? `imaging_fast` computes the SAME path (same events, same trust-region iteration, same stopping tests) with a cheaper arithmetic: the Gram matrix of the Jacobian and an n x n eigendecomposition in place of the SVD of the (N + n) x n augmented Jacobian, batched separable-kernel products, an incremental model frame (cost per step independent of the number of units), a cheaper lattice correlation, and an EXACT early exit (the path ends at the first entrant below the significance threshold of the `snr` rule, which is the block `stop_snr` returns anyway). (Q2) Is its output the same? (Q3) What is the cost ratio to Picasso now, honestly, and how does the cost scale with the number of emitters and the frame size?

Not attempted in this plan (declared): a coarse-to-fine dictionary lattice (a different algorithm, would need its own equivalence study; if run later it is labelled POST HOC), numba (installable: `pip download numba` succeeds through the allowed hosts; not used, because the profile is dominated by BLAS, erf/exp and eigh, not by interpreter loops), float32, frame-level parallelism (the 4-core machine is shared; any parallel number would be a footnote only and is not measured). Everything is single-process, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`, `nice`.

## 2. What is measured

**T. Per-frame cost, SMLM.** The a priori configuration of the main results (`exp_smlm_iss.DEFAULT`: halo model `hp5h`, 5 px high-pass, significance 5, tolerance 1e-8, 200 evaluations; sliding widths 1.0-3.2 px; label-free calibration `get_cal`) on the four 2D challenge sets MT0.N1.LD, MT0.N1.HD, MT0.N2.LD, MT0.N2.HD. Frames: 24 evenly spaced DEVELOPMENT frames per set (LD: stack indices 0-999; HD: 0-99; `evenly_spaced`). Per frame and per engine (original `exp_smlm_iss.run_frame`; new `smlm_fast.run_frame_fast`), 3 repeats, interleaved (old, new, old, new, ...), the minimum over the repeats of the process CPU time (`time.process_time`; the wall time is stored too); the machine is shared with other jobs, so a CPU-time minimum is the estimate that is least affected. Picasso: the R2E rung of each set (the options in `results/smlm/picasso_ladder_raw.npz`, `<set>|R2E|config`: identification box, fit box, Gaussian filter, padding, fit method, as tuned on the development frames by the existing ladder; the offset correction "+c" is a subtraction of a constant and costs nothing), Picasso 0.11.3 in the interpreter named by `SMLM_PICASSO_PYTHON`, identification + fit of each of the same 24 frames separately, after a JIT warm-up on 8 other frames, 3 repeats, minimum of the process CPU time and of the wall time. Reported per set: mean seconds per frame of each engine; ratios old/new, new/Picasso, old/Picasso as ratio of means over the 24 frames with a 95 % percentile bootstrap interval over frames (1000 resamples, seed 0, resampling frames with replacement; the frames are 4 to 40 apart in the recording, so they are treated as independent), and the median of the per-frame ratios old/new. The headline numbers are the pooled ones over the four sets (mean of the four sets' means).

**S. Scaling, synthetic.** The same fit (hp5h default, significance 5, sliding widths) on simulated EM-CCD frames (`imaging.simulate_emccd`, 1500 photons per emitter, background and noise law of MT0.N1.LD's label-free calibration, emitters uniform in the frame at distance >= 3 px from the border): (S1) 64 x 64 frames with K = 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 emitters; (S2) frames of side 32, 48, 64, 96, 128 at constant density (K = round(6 n^2 / 4096), at least 1). 3 frames per setting (2 for the 128 side), 2 repeats, CPU-time minimum, both engines. The exponent is the slope of log time on log K (S1) and log (pixels) (S2) by least squares with a bootstrap interval over frames; it is reported for each engine and for old/new.

**A. Astronomy, development patches.** The tuned configuration of the frozen astronomy study (`hp 3`, `r_ex 1.2`, `exp_astro_m2.run_path`, all its settings: three-part PSF fitted to the patch's PSF stamp, lattice 0.5 px, margin 2, fit radius 4, tolerance 1e-6, time stop with thresholds 2 ... 8 and the BIC block, cleanup) on the two development patches (110, 630) and (210, 630) of M2. The original is `imaging_guard.sliding_path_guarded` (the guarded path of the corrected study), the new is `imaging_fast.sliding_path_fast` with `cycle_guard=True`, both through `exp_astro_m2.run_path` with the path function replaced in the worker process (as `exp_astro_cyclefix.py` does; the frozen files are not modified). (A1) Paths truncated at 25, 50, 100, 200, 400 entries (the entry cap set to that number in the worker process): CPU time of the whole `run_path` (min of 2 repeats), seconds per entry against the number of entries (the asymptotics in the number of units), and the equivalence below. (A2) The complete path of patch (110, 630) once for each engine (it is long: about 1300 entries; the original takes tens of minutes), CPU time and equivalence of the detections at every threshold.

## 3. What counts as equivalent (fixed now)

The new path is NOT bit-identical: the trust-region step is computed from a Gram matrix instead of an SVD, the model is carried incrementally, sums are re-ordered. It is declared equivalent to the original on a set of frames/patches if ALL of:
* SMLM (default configuration, development frames: LD 150 evenly spaced frames per set, HD 40 per set; plus the variants battery on 12 frames per set: configuration `hp3` with significance 7, `greedy` order, no width sliding, a second rule list ('snr', 'bic') which switches the early exit off): the number of detections is the same on every frame; the detections, paired by minimum-cost assignment, differ by at most 0.1 nm in x and y, by a relative 1e-3 in photons and by 1e-3 px in width; the scored metrics (Jaccard, RMSE, efficiency E, pooled over the frames, and their paired difference with the paired block bootstrap interval of `smlm_common.bootstrap_diff_ci`, 1000 resamples) differ by at most 1e-3 in E (percentage points) and the interval of E_new - E_old contains 0. The largest observed differences are reported whether or not they pass.
* Astronomy (the two development patches): in the truncated paths the sequence of events (entries and exits, lattice indices) is identical, the entry times agree to a relative 1e-6, the barred set of the guard is identical; the detections at every threshold (and the BIC block) have the same count, positions within 1e-3 px, fluxes within a relative 1e-3.
* The limit-cycle guard cases (`tests/test_imaging_fast.py`): the planted-cycle worlds of `tests/test_imaging_guard.py` give the same events and the same `barred` list through `sliding_path_fast` as through `sliding_path_guarded`, `cycle_guard=False` agrees with `imaging.sliding_path`, and the early exit returns a prefix of the full path with the identical chosen block.
A frame or patch that fails is reported as a failure with its numbers; the words "same output" are used only for what passes.

## 4. What the report may say, and what would change it

Verdict on speed (primary): the ratio old/new, pooled over the four sets, per set and with its interval, from CPU time. "At least 10x" is stated for a set only if the lower end of its 95 % interval is >= 10. The cost ratio to Picasso is stated as new/Picasso (and old/Picasso) per set and pooled, whatever it is: if it is still orders of magnitude, the report says so in those words. The early exit is part of the new path; its share is measured separately (the same engine with `early=False`, 24 frames per set, reported in the same table), so that a reader can tell the exact-algebra speed-up from the exact early exit. The speed-ups hold for the rule 'snr' with threshold >= 3 (the early exit); for other rules the early exit is off and the table says which.

What would make this a null: the new path failing the equivalence on a non-negligible share of frames (then the fast evaluation is a declared variant with its own accuracy study, and the ratios are reported as the cost of that variant), or a ratio below 2 on a set. No result of this plan is a claim about the accuracy of the ISS against Picasso; it changes no F or E number of any earlier report.

## 5. Addendum (written before any run of the final script; same date)

Part TT: the timing of section 2-T is repeated on 12 evenly spaced development frames per set for the E-TUNED configuration of the main study (the operating point `choose_op(op_table(...), 'E')` of `exp_smlm_iss`: configuration and significance threshold; the width bound and the offset are post-processing, free), a single threshold, same engines, same repeats, same statistic. Reason: the headline numbers of the paper (`iss.md`: 0.5 to 6.5 s per frame) are for those configurations, and the threshold sweep of that report keeps the whole tail (no early exit possible); the table reports the early-exit-free column next to the other. Picasso's cost there is the mean of its 24 frames of section 2-T. Equivalence in this part is checked by the detection counts of the old and the new engine on each of the 12 frames only (the tuned configuration is not part of the positions/photons battery); a frame with unequal counts is reported as such.

## 6. Post hoc additions (written after the first results; NOT part of the pre-registration)

After the timing and equivalence parts had run, the astronomy table showed FAILS against the section-3 tolerances for truncated paths of 400 entries, and the complete path of patch (110, 630) had different events. Three parts were therefore added, each labelled POST HOC in the report: AC (the original guarded path against itself with a ten times smaller block-fit tolerance, at 200 and 400 entries: how far the ORIGINAL moves under a tiny perturbation), AX (the complete paths of both development patches by the original, the new engine and the tight-tolerance original, scored by the F-measure at mag < 22 with a paired block bootstrap: the equivalence study of the declared variant), ENV (CPU model and the load average sampled every 30 s beside the timing parts). The section-3 verdicts (including the FAILs) are reported as they came out; no tolerance was changed. The coordinator's suggestion to run the timing battery on a private cloud CPU notebook was not taken: all three methods (original, new, Picasso) were timed in the same session on this one machine, interleaved, with the load recorded; no timing of one machine enters a ratio with another.
