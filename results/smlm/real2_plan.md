# Pre-registration: the second real-data study of the sliding ISS against Picasso (fresh frames, a registration gate, a repaired tuning grid, and an overlap test)

**Declared: 2026-11-09 14:32 UTC** (written before any localizer of either method was run on any fresh test frame, before any pore reference of the fresh sets was built, before any registration shift of a fresh set was computed, and before any fresh table was opened; the commit that contains this file precedes `experiments/exp_smlm_real2.py`'s test step). Author: the workstream-A agent of the improvement round. Nothing below has been scored.

## 0. Why this study exists, and everything that was looked at before this declaration

The first pre-registered pore study (`results/smlm/real_gt_plan.md`, `real_gt.md`) ended in a null: the ISS returned more localizations than Picasso but no narrower ring at equal yield (-0.2 [-1.7, 1.3] nm on Nup96), one dataset (D1) was void because the published table is shifted by 200 to 340 nm against the raw frames (the plan had no registration gate), and the ISS development optimum sat at the TOP of the kappa grid (kappa = 10) on D1. This study asks whether that null is an artefact of an unequal tuning grid and of a metric that cannot see what the ISS could win, and tests it once on frames that nobody has scored.

**Looked at on DEVELOPMENT frames only** (D1 frames 1-300; D2 field 17 frames 1-300 in the window of the first study), for the diagnosis of the grid-edge optimum (these are the numbers `experiments/exp_smlm_real2.py --step diag` recomputes and prints): (a) the label-free noise law is NOT too small. On the first 60 (D1) / 100 (D2) development frames the robust standard deviation of the standardized residual of the kappa-4 block, away from every fitted unit, is 0.905 (D1) and 0.897 (D2): the law is about 10 % too large, the opposite of what a kappa pushed to the top of its grid would need. The residual has a heavy POSITIVE tail only (D1: P(r > 4 sd) 7.9e-4 against 3.2e-5 for a Gaussian, P(r < -4 sd) 0): unfitted signal, not noise. (b) The fraction of the units that lie on the reference ring rises with the unit's amplitude and with its significance (D1: 58 % in the lowest amplitude fifth, 70 % in the highest; the ring is 25 % of the disc area), and the units that are far from any pore are dim in D2 (ring fraction 0.26-0.31 for the lower four amplitude fifths, 0.58 in the highest). The ISS grid of the first study had two dials (kappa, an upper width limit), Picasso's tuned pipeline (R2E) had four (net gradient, minimum photons, minimum and maximum width). With the ISS given the three missing post-filters of the same kind (a minimum amplitude and a minimum width; the maximum width it already had) the ISS development optimum is no longer at the edge (D1: kappa 15 of {4,...,50}) and its development E_s is 50.5 against 50.0 (Picasso R2E) on D1 and 18.8 against 19.4 on D2 (exploratory single-configuration runs, hp3h_s12 for D1, hp5_r25 for D2). So the grid-edge optimum was an artefact of the asymmetric dials, not of the noise calibration; this plan removes the asymmetry. (c) Development frontiers (ring yield against off-ring count when the post-filters vary, both methods; in-sample, with the ISS offered more points): D1 ISS 1129 vs Picasso 1144 at an off-ring count of 911, ISS 456 vs 370 at 344; D2 ISS 187 vs Picasso 188 at 323, ISS 174 vs 147 at 98. The ISS ring widths along the frontier are 1 to 3 nm narrower in D1 (selection effect of the width window, unverified). (d) A superposition prototype of the overlap test of Part B on D2 development frames (K = 2 and K = 4 frames added): recall of bright reference events and recovery of reference pairs closer than 2 sigma0 are the same for both methods to the third digit (K = 2: ISS 0.64 / Picasso 0.61-0.65 recall, close-pair recovery 0.454 / 0.456; K = 4: 0.50 / 0.50, 0.287 / 0.293). The same prototype on D1 development frames (K = 3): at the lowest net gradient of Picasso's scaled grid (61.5 detections per merged frame) Picasso recovers 0.43 of the bright reference events with 19 unconfirmed detections per frame, the ISS at kappa 10 without further filter 0.46 with 24 (kappa 4: 0.61 with 51; Picasso cannot be pushed further because its candidate set stops at that threshold), and the close-pair recall is 0.24 (Picasso) against 0.25 to 0.33 (ISS); that is, at K = 3 on D1 the ISS may be ahead at the high-density end, a prototype with the development frames, one tuning point per method, no post-filter ascent, and the first prototype of D1 used the D2 Picasso parameters by mistake and was repeated with the right ones. Expectation, stated in advance: Part A probably ties or gives a small ISS gain; Part B may give a small ISS gain on D1 (the denser recording) and probably ties on D2.

**Looked at on fresh data before this declaration**: (i) the sizes and md5 of the new files (field 19 of Zenodo 3540443: the first 400,000,000 bytes of piece 1 = 1055 whole frames, and its 55 MB SMAP table); nothing in them was opened. ## 2. Registration gate (new; the first study had none)

Pore centres and the reference table live in the authors' drift-corrected coordinates; raw-frame localizations do not. For every test set, before any localizer is scored:

* **Shift curve from the raw data, not from any localizer.** For each block of 125 consecutive frames: the temporal median of the block's frames is subtracted from each frame, the positive parts are summed, and the sum is cross-correlated (after a Gaussian high-pass of 6 px and a smoothing of 1 px) with the rendering of the published table of the same frames (on the pixel grid, Gaussian-blurred by sigma0 = 1.6 px (D1) / 1.33 px (D2), the table in the localizers' corner convention of the first study); the correlation peak within +-8 px is refined by a parabola. A cubic polynomial in the frame index (iterated once with a 2.5-sigma clip of the block shifts) is fitted per axis to the block shifts of a test set; the per-frame shift s(f) is its value. Every localization of every method (not the published rows) of frame f is translated by -s(f). Part B converts the table's reference positions into the raw system instead (adds s(f)), with the same curve.
* **Gate R1 (per set; fails = the set is VOID, reported, not scored for the verdict):** the largest deviation of a block shift from the fitted curve is at most 20 nm on each axis, and the peak-over-standard-deviation quality of every block is at least 2.5.
* **Gate R2 (per row, after the shift, computed from the stored localizations):** for the blocks of 250 frames, the translation that maps the row's localizations onto the published table (the first study's `block_shift`) has a median modulus of at most 15 nm and a 90th percentile of at most 30 nm, for each of R0, R2E and the ISS rows. If a set passes R1 but a row fails R2 the set is void for the verdict and the failing row is reported.
* D2's shift is expected to be a few nm (the first study); it is estimated and applied in the same way.

## 3. Calibration, comparators, tuning (development frames only; same objective, same data for both methods)

* **Calibration, label-free**, on the frames of each set (development sets: as stored): background surface, noise law, sigma0 of the ISS, from the frames and Picasso-R0 localizations as in `results/smlm/real_frc.md` (`exp_smlm_real.calibrate`). Camera constants for Picasso: D1 real, D2 nominal (as in the first study).
* **Picasso**: R0 (defaults), R1E (net gradient tuned) and R2E (every option tuned), EXACTLY the three frozen configurations of the first study (tuned for E_s on the development frames, 11309 (D1) / 7813 (D2) configurations scored). They are not re-tuned. The comparator of the rule is R2E.
* **ISS (repaired grid, "ISS4")**: the dual-order guarded ISS (`imaging_guard.sliding_path_guarded`, unchanged), the first study's 8 model configurations per dataset (D1: hp3, hp3h, hp5, hp5h, then the four variants of the best base hp3h: _s12, _s17, _r15, _r25 as in `real_gt.md`; D2: hp3, hp3h, hp5, hp5h and the four variants _s12, _s17, _r15, _r25 of hp5, the first study's best base). For each configuration the path is computed once per frame and read at 10 significance thresholds kappa in {4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 50}. Four dials, a coordinate ascent that mirrors Picasso's (`exp_smlm_real_gt.ascent`: strict improvement, the dials in the order below, at most 3 rounds), started at (kappa 5, no amplitude floor, no lower width, upper width 3.2):
  1. kappa in the ten values above;
  2. an amplitude floor fw x w_ref with fw in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3}, w_ref = the median fitted amplitude of the kappa-10 detections of the frames being localized (label-free: computed on the frames of the set itself);
  3. a lower width limit smin x s with smin in {none, 1.0, 1.02, 1.05, 1.1, 1.15, 1.2, 1.3}, s = sigma0 / 1.4 px (the first study's scale);
  4. an upper width limit smax x s with smax in {1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.5, 3.2}.
  The objective is E_s on the development frames (the first study's, same pores, same n_exp). The configuration, kappa, fw, smin, smax with the largest development E_s (ties: the earlier configuration, then the larger kappa) are frozen. The number of operating points scored is reported next to Picasso's. The same frozen ISS4 point is run on both D2 test sets (the D2 configuration), on T1 the D1 one.
* The first study's frozen ISS choice (two dials) is also run on the fresh frames when its model configuration coincides with ISS4's or the set is D2 (otherwise skipped: it costs a second path run on D1), as a continuity row, not in the rule.
* The frozen choices, the scale factors and the code commit are written to the raw and hashed (sha256) BEFORE the first test frame is localized; the hash and the UTC times are printed in the report. The test run is one run per test set; a repeat is allowed only to repair a crash that leaves the numbers unchanged, and every execution is listed.

## 4. Part A (primary): the pore geometry, tuned and frozen points compared at their own tradeoff

* **Scoring** is the first study's, unchanged in form: each localization is assigned to the nearest reference pore (the T_ref of section 1, the pores re-derived by the first study's finder with the first study's thresholds, accepted and merged by the first study's rules), the radial profile of the localizations within R_ref + 150 nm (D1) / + 100 nm (D2) is fitted by a ring and a uniform floor (Poisson maximum likelihood), J_s = N / (N + floor + missed yield), E_s = 100 - sqrt((100 - 100 J_s)^2 + w^2) with w the ring width in nm, the missed yield from n_exp = the T_ref ring yield scaled to the number of scored frames. The coordinate convention of the localizers against the table is the first study's, measured on the development frames (D1: none; D2: half a pixel, -53 nm), not re-measured.
* **Primary outcome**, per test dataset: Delta E_s = E_s(ISS4, frozen) - E_s(Picasso R2E, frozen), on the registered localizations. 95 % percentile intervals of 1000 paired block-bootstrap resamples (blocks of 10 consecutive frames, the same blocks for every row, seed 0; the radial fit redone in each replicate; for pooled D2 the blocks of each member are resampled independently). Sensitivity: the same with the pores resampled (seed 1). A claim must hold for both.
* **Decision rule A** (Delta E_s only; the ring width is no longer a co-primary outcome: in the development frames w varies by 19 to 27 nm across operating points of both methods because the ring itself is 20 nm wide, so a difference of one or two nm cannot be told from a threshold effect, and the first study's equal-yield thinning is by random deletion, which penalises a method that returns a wider range of amplitudes; both are reported as secondary):
  * **ISS better** on a test dataset: the interval of Delta E_s lies above 0. **A real-data advantage** may be written only if this holds on BOTH T1 and pooled D2, both valid under section 2 (and the pore-resampling intervals agree).
  * **ISS worse**: the upper limit of Delta E_s is below 0 on at least one valid dataset and the ISS is better on neither.
  * **Equivalent**: both intervals lie within +-3 E points (the first study's margin) and neither excludes 0.
  * **Mixed** otherwise (reported as such: the real-data sentence is limited to the dataset that holds).
  * A win in which the ISS returns more localizations AND a larger off-ring count so that its ring yield at the off-ring count of Picasso R2E (read on the ISS's own curve in kappa, all other dials frozen, by log-linear interpolation; and the same read for Picasso on its net-gradient curve) is NOT larger than Picasso's is reported as "tradeoff, not a better localizer" and does not count as an advantage. The interval of that equal-off-ring yield ratio is a secondary outcome with the same blocks.
* **Secondary, described not tested**: J_s, w, N, floor, radius bias, ring localizations per pore per 1000 frames, the first study's equal-yield width, the C8 corner contrast (D2), the fraction of duplicate localizations (pairs within sigma0 in one frame), seconds per frame, Picasso R0 and R1E rows, the continuity ISS row, the published-table rows, the frontier curves in kappa and in net gradient, the registered and the unregistered (shift not applied) analyses side by side.

## 5. Part B (second question, own rule): overlapping emitters at a density made from the data

A claim about resolving overlapping emitters needs overlapping emitters. The recordings are sparse (D1 about 26, the D2 windows about 5 to 12 localizations per frame), so overlaps are rare. Part B manufactures them from real frames with real PSFs, camera noise and background: **K = 3** frames are added pixel by pixel and the per-pixel temporal median of the whole set's frames (the background and offset) is subtracted twice, so the merged frame is what the camera would have recorded had the emitters of the three frames been on at once. In each block of 21 consecutive test frames (positions 0 to 20 of the set), the groups are {i, i + 7, i + 14}, i = 0..6 (frames 7 apart are far enough in time that an emitter that is on in one is rarely on in the next); leftover frames are dropped. T1: 665 groups, T2a: 350, T2b: 245.

* **Reference events** are the published table's localizations of the three constituent frames (in raw coordinates by the section 2 curve), inside the scored region and not within 200 nm of its border; "bright" reference events have a table photon count at or above the MEDIAN of the table's photon counts in the development frames of that dataset (a constant frozen at development: D1 and D2 values printed). Every merged frame therefore has a known set of emitter positions, as good as the table's.
* **Matching** is one to one (the Hungarian assignment on the distances, a pair must be closer than r_m = 0.5 sigma0 of the set (about 80 nm in D1, 70 nm in D2, from the label-free single-frame calibration)). A detection matched to a bright reference event is a TP; a detection matched to no reference event of either kind is a FP ("unconfirmed"; real emitters absent from the table count against both methods alike); a bright reference event matched to no detection is a FN. **J_B = TP / (TP + FP + FN)**.
* **Close pairs**: the bright reference events that have another bright reference event of a DIFFERENT constituent frame within 2 sigma0 (overlap by construction). Close-pair recall = the fraction of those events that are matched; pair resolution = the fraction of such pairs of which both members are matched.
* **Tuning** (development frames 1-300 of D1 and of D2 field 17, groups built the same way: 98 each): the model configuration of each method is frozen from section 3 (ISS: the ISS4 configuration; Picasso: the structure of R2E); the post-filters are re-ascended for **J_B** on the merged development frames with the same grids, the same ascent and the same rounds as in section 3 (Picasso: net gradient, minimum photons, minimum and maximum width, the scaled grids of the first study; ISS: the four dials). Frozen and hashed before the test merge is localized. Rows: Picasso R0 (defaults), Picasso "R2B" (re-tuned), ISS "ISS-B" (re-tuned).
* **Primary outcome of Part B**: Delta J_B = J_B(ISS-B) - J_B(R2B), per test dataset (T1; pooled D2), 95 % block-bootstrap intervals (blocks of 10 consecutive groups). **Decision rule B**: ISS better = the interval lies above 0 on both datasets (and the close-pair recall difference is not negative on either); worse = the upper limit below 0 on a dataset and no win; equivalent = both intervals within +-3 J points and neither excludes 0; mixed otherwise. Part B is a separate question from Part A: its rule is evaluated on its own; nothing is pooled with Part A.
* Weaknesses: the merged frames are a manufactured regime (the camera noise adds in variance, not as an EM-CCD would at the higher signal; the table is a ThunderSTORM / SMAP localization with the authors' filters, a reference of its own, not the emitters' true positions); close pairs are the minority (a few percent of reference events at K = 3); a method that is better calibrated to the authors' filters scores better for reasons unrelated to resolving power. Because of this the Part B verdict can support the words "on frames made by adding three real frames", never "on real overlapping emitters".

## 6. Gates, drops and what the outcomes mean

* Validity gates V1 (R_ref from the odd and even 100-frame blocks of T_ref within 2 nm) and V4 (at least 100 scored pores per test set), and V3 for D2 (at least 50 % of the pores have a significant 8th harmonic: used only for the C8 secondary), as in the first study, evaluated on T_ref before any localizer runs; the R_ref of the two D2 fields within 3 nm of each other (gate V2 without the literature value); the registration gates R1 and R2 of section 2. A set that fails a gate is void and reported; it is neither evidence for nor against the ISS.
* **Dropped**: if the ISS cannot be run as specified on a test set (a path hitting the cap of 400 entries in more than 5 % of the frames, or the run exceeding 12 CPU hours), if a leak is found (any frame of the development or first-test ranges used here as a test frame, or a T_ref frame inside a test range), or if the frozen hash does not match: the corresponding verdict is declared void.
* **If both rules give a tie or a loss**, the paper's real-data sentence is the null of the first study plus this null. **If rule A holds on both datasets**, the paper may say, with the caveats of section 1 (one field per dataset for D1, three recordings for D2, no between-microscope variance, a reference derived from third-party tables of other frames of the same recordings): "on frames not used for any choice, the ISS with the same family of post-filters as Picasso's reaches a higher E_s on registered localizations of nuclear-pore rings". Status words: PRE-REGISTERED HELD-OUT; anything computed after the test result is labelled POST HOC.
* Descriptive, after the rule is read and labelled POST HOC: per-block stability, the dependence of Delta E_s on the amplitude floor, and (if the machine allows) the ISS greedy-order row.

## Amendment 2 (declared 2026-11-09 15:27 UTC, before the development tuning of Part B and before any test frame was localized)

A rehearsal of the pipeline on 63-frame stand-ins (outputs discarded) showed that for Part B the Picasso structure fixed to the first study's R2E (tuned for the pore E_s) can score a lower development J_B than Picasso's plain defaults (0.378 against 0.386 on D1).
