Generated by `experiments/exp_smlm_decode.py --from-raw` from `decode_raw.npz`; every number below is printed by the script from that file (the ground truth, the candidate detections of DECODE on the development and the test frames and the detections of the comparison rows are stored in it; the operating points are recomputed from the candidates and checked against the stored test detections). Frame indices are 0-based stack indices, positions in nm (corner convention), metrics pooled over the frames with 95 % intervals of a bootstrap in blocks of 10 consecutive frames (1000 resamples, seed 0); differences are paired (the same resampled blocks). ## Summary: efficiency E and Jaccard index J on the test frames (point estimates; intervals in the per-set tables)

## Protocol and deviations

* The plan (`decode_run_plan.md`) was written before the run (about 09:22 UTC on 2026-11-09; the run was submitted at 09:23:25 UTC). Checkpoint, axis convention, probability threshold and offset were chosen on the development frames only; the test frames were run once, with the selected snapshot. One training run (one configuration) per set.
* Inputs of DECODE: the camera constants of the dataset pages (QE 0.90 set; DECODE's default is 1), the quiet-frame background, the photon statistics and the 97 % |z| quantile of the development ground truth of the set, the emitter density of the set, and a PSF calibration built from the radial single-emitter profile of the development frames of MT0.N1.LD (51 x 51 px, 41 z cells of 50 nm, 1.42 photons per ground-truth photon, symmetric). A symmetric PSF model measured on 360 single-emitter frames is a handicap that a bead calibration would not have; the HD sets reuse the PSF and the background of the LD sets of their noise class.
* Deviations from `decode_run_spec.md`: (1) the pinned commit needs torch 1.7.1 (its `environment.yaml`): used, with cuda 11.0, on Tesla T4 GPUs; (2) the spec's `PostProcessingParam.raw_th: 0.05` is not read by the commit's `infer.py` (it hard-codes 0.1): the run uses DECODE's own inference pipeline in a script that copies `infer.py` with the threshold 0.05; (3) the PSF is option 1 of the spec in a radially symmetric form; (4) axial positions uniform on [0, 600] nm (the PSF is even in z); (5) `max_number_targets` 40 / 60 instead of 250; (6) the learning rate decays by a factor 10 over the wall-clock budget instead of DECODE's step schedule, and training is stopped by the clock; (7) the x and y of DECODE are the row and column axes (x_nm = (y_px + 0.5) 100): the run chose the convention on the development frames and recorded the alternative.
* Not comparable with the leaderboard results of DECODE's authors (hidden test data of the challenge; the MT0 sets are simulated training sets that share one structure of three microtubules and are not independent datasets). A network trained on emitters scattered uniformly over the field has not seen the microtubule structure; the ISS and Picasso do not use it either.
* Cost: the ISS needs no training and ~1 s (LD) to ~5 s (HD) per frame on one CPU core; DECODE trained for 4942-4994 s (36/37/36/37 epochs of 10,000 simulated frames) per set here (hours on a GPU in the spec) and then fits a frame in milliseconds on a GPU.
