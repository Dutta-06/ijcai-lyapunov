**What this measures and what it does not.** The ground truth is the geometry of a nuclear pore (a ring of radius R about 87 nm for gp210 in D1, about 53 nm for Nup96 in D2), not the emitters: which emitters are on in a frame is unknown, labelling is incomplete, and the pore centres, radii and 8-fold phases are taken from the authors' localization tables of OTHER frames of the same field. A localizer is scored by how well its localizations of the held-out frames lie on the reference ring (a Poisson fit of the pooled radial profile: ring width w, ring yield N, uniform floor), by J_s = N / (N + floor + missed yield) and E_s = 100 - sqrt((100 - 100 J_s)^2 + (alpha w)^2), alpha = 1 per nm. It is **not** a measurement of detection accuracy per molecule, and it covers the frames and pores of ONE field of view per test set (no between-field variance). No raw DNA-origami movie with a designed spacing was publicly available, so no origami test was made; D3 (SIMFLUX) is not analysed in this report.

* One field of view per test set; intervals cover frames (and, in the sensitivity, pores) of that field only. The reference localizer (the authors' software) and the pore model are shared by all rows; the "as published" rows are not comparators.
* D3 (SIMFLUX nanorulers) is outside the inference and not analysed here.
