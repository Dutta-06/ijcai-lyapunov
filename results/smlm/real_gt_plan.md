# Pre-registration: the sliding ISS against Picasso on real SMLM recordings with a known geometry

**Declared: 2026-11-09 07:56 UTC** (written before any run of the ISS on any real recording of these datasets and before any localization of either method was scored; to be committed before `experiments/exp_smlm_realgt.py`, which does not exist yet, is written or run). Author: the data-engineering agent that surveyed the data (`data/smlm/real_candidates.md`, `data/smlm/fetch_real_gt.py`, `data/smlm/checksums_real_gt.json`, `results/smlm/real_gt_usability.json`). It is a PLAN for the coordinator's review; nothing below has been scored.

**What was looked at before this declaration (full disclosure).** Frame sizes, dark levels, counts of Picasso 0.11.3 default-pipeline localizations on 100 to 200 frames per dataset (to see that the data are readable; no scoring, no tuning), a few rendered frames and renderings of the published localization tables, and a crude ring finder run on the published tables of the WHOLE recordings (all frames, including the frames that are reserved below as development and test frames) to see that pores are present and what their radii are (D1 about 86 to 87 nm, D2 about 51 to 52 nm). The numbers of that ring finder are feasibility facts; the reference of the plan is re-derived below from reserved reference frames only. **The ISS has not been run on any of these data. No localization of Picasso on these data has been compared with any model.**

## 1. Datasets and what each can and cannot show

| id | data | geometry (ground truth) | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Zenodo 5068525 (Heil et al. 2018): Xenopus nuclear pores, gp210-AF647, STORM on glass, 20000 frames of 117 x 118 px, 102 nm pixel, EM gain 100, 15.01 photoelectrons/ADU, offset 100 ADU; the authors' ThunderSTORM table (517,370 localizations) | a ring of radius R about 87 nm (estimated from the data; hundreds of pores in one field of view); 8-fold sub-structure not resolved | primary |
| D2 | Zenodo 3540443 (Thevathasan et al. 2019): Nup96-SNAP-AF647, 2D STORM, 106 nm pixel, 419 x 419 px frames, fields of view 17 and 18, first 1055 frames of each (prefix of the 4.3 GB piece); the authors' SMAP tables of the whole recordings (30000 frames) | Nup96 reference standard: 8-fold, ring diameter about 107 nm (R about 53 nm), two rings about 50 nm apart (literature value from memory, not verifiable here: nature.com is blocked); measured 51 to 52 nm in the SMAP tables | primary |
| D3 | Zenodo 7771932 (SIMFLUX 80 nm DNA-PAINT nanorulers), first 923 frames | designed site spacing 80 nm (title only) | exploratory, outside the registered claim |

What this test can show: whether, on real frames of structures of known geometry, localizations of the ISS agree better with that geometry than those of a tuned Picasso at equal tuning effort. What it cannot show: (a) per-molecule detection accuracy (the emitters that are on in a frame are not known; labelling is incomplete); (b) generalisation across microscopes (two camera types, one lab each); (c) anything about a DNA-origami grid, ruler or Picasso origami sample: **no raw-frame origami dataset with a designed spacing and several fields of view could be obtained** (`real_candidates.md`); the requested origami test is NOT part of this plan; (d) between-field-of-view variance: D1 has ONE field of view and D2's held-out test is ONE field of view, so every interval below covers frames (and pores) of that field only. Conclusions are worded "on these fields of view".

Camera knowledge: D1 has the full calibration (an EM-CCD; QE is not given and is irrelevant to localization). D2's files do NOT give gain, offset or e-/ADU (Photometrics-type sCMOS, `CIS2020F`); D3 gives none and not even the pixel size. The ISS therefore works in raw ADU with the label-free noise law of `results/smlm/real_frc.md` (`calibrate_noise`, PSF width sigma0 from bright isolated Picasso-R0 fits), calibrated on the frames of the field to be scored without any label (labels do not exist for these frames); Picasso receives D1's real camera constants and for D2 nominal ones (baseline 100, gain 1, sensitivity 1, QE 0.95: they change only its photon column, as shown in `real_frc.md`).

## 2. Splits (everything disjoint in frames, and in pores for D1)

All frame numbers below are 1-based as in the published tables; the stack index is the frame number minus 1.

**D1 (Heil, glass).** Reference table T_ref = the ThunderSTORM table restricted to frames 2001 to 10000 and 12001 to 20000 (16000 frames), used ONLY to define the pores (section 3). Development frames: 1 to 300; test frames: 10001 to 12000. Development pores: reference pores with a centre at x < 5800 nm; test pores: centre at x > 6200 nm (a guard band of 400 nm; x in the table's nm coordinates). Development and test therefore share no frame, no pore and no pixel of the field except the guard band. The comparison is made on the test frames and test pores only.

**D2 (Nup96).** Development: field 17, frames 1 to 300 (calibration of the ISS noise law and sigma0 uses all 1055 frames of the field: no labels are involved). Test: field 18, frames 1 to 1055 (calibration label-free on its own frames). Reference table T_ref = the SMAP table of the same field restricted to frames 1057 to 30000 (frame 1056 is the cut frame of the prefix and is dropped). Both fields have the same sample, microscope and day; they are different cells (the Zenodo description and the file names disagree on the cell line, see `real_candidates.md`).

Windows (compute): the ISS on 419 x 419 px frames is not affordable (it cost 7.8 s CPU per 128 x 128 frame on the 2013 tubulin set). For D2 both methods are scored in ONE 128 x 128 px window per field, chosen from T_ref alone as the window containing the largest number of accepted reference pores, before any localizer output exists. D1 frames (117 x 118 px) are used whole. The ISS cost per frame is measured on 20 development frames before the test run; if (cost x frames) exceeds 12 CPU hours for D1 test, the test frames are reduced to the nested subset 10001 to 11000 (declared now; the Picasso side always uses the same frames). The 300 development frames are likewise the nested subset of the first N_dev frames that fit the same budget (the same for both methods).

## 3. Ground-truth model and its construction (before any localizer is run on development or test frames)

1. **Reference pores from T_ref only.** A ring matched filter on a 10 nm histogram of T_ref (ring radius start value 8 px (D1) / 5 px (D2), Gaussian ring profile of relative width 0.2), local maxima above the 95th percentile of the occupied pixels (D1) / 97th (D2) as candidate centres, three iterations of an algebraic circle fit on T_ref localizations in the annulus 40 to 125 nm (D1) / 25 to 80 nm (D2) around each centre. A pore is accepted if the annulus holds at least 15 T_ref localizations and the fitted radius lies within +-25 % of the median of all candidates; centres closer than 1.5 x R_start (R_start in histogram pixels) are merged. The finder is `rings()` of `experiments/real_gt_usability_check.py` plus the acceptance and merging rule just stated; the thresholds are fixed now (the usability check showed that the NUMBER of candidates depends strongly on the threshold, from 157 to 647 in D1 and 1700 to 6100 in D2, so the count of pores is not a result; the accepted list is frozen once). The list of accepted pores (centre, fitted radius, annulus counts) is written to a file whose sha256 is recorded before the first run of any localizer on development frames; it is never edited afterwards.
2. **Ring radius.** R_ref = the median fitted radius of the accepted pores, per dataset (per field for D2; the development value is used for the development field and the test value for the test field).
3. **8-fold phase (D2 only).** For each accepted pore, the phase phi_j of the 8th azimuthal harmonic of the T_ref localizations within 25 nm of the ring (maximum of the harmonic over phi in [0, 45 degrees)).
4. **Validity gates (evaluated on T_ref, before any run; if one fails the dataset is declared void for the corresponding outcome, which is NOT evidence for or against the ISS).** (V1) R_ref estimated separately from the odd and the even 100-frame blocks of T_ref differ by at most 2 nm (D1 and D2). (V2) D2: R_ref(field 17) and R_ref(field 18) differ by at most 3 nm, and both lie within 5 % of 53.5 nm (the literature radius; if it does not, the pixel size or the model is questioned, and both methods would be affected identically, so only the absolute radius outcome is voided). (V3) D2: at least 50 % of the accepted pores have a significant 8th harmonic (permutation p < 0.05 against random phase); otherwise the 8-fold outcome S3 is voided. (V4) at least 100 accepted pores in the scored region of each dataset.

Radial coordinate of a localization p: assign it to the nearest accepted pore centre c_j (Voronoi) and compute r = |p - c_j|; only localizations with r < R_ref + 150 nm (D1) / R_ref + 100 nm (D2) enter (the "pore disc"). Pores at the border of the scored region (disc not fully inside) are dropped.

## 4. Scoring metrics (computed per method on the scored frames and scored pores)

* **Radial profile fit.** The pooled binned counts of r over all scored pores (5 nm bins) are fitted by Poisson maximum likelihood with f(r) = N_ring x Normal(r; mu, w) + b x 2 pi r (a ring and a uniform floor, which absorbs background and chance coincidences with neighbouring rings, important in D1 where the pores almost touch). Outputs: N_ring (ring localizations), mu (fitted radius), w (radial width, the effective radial precision together with the intrinsic ring width), N_floor = b x disc area, and the number of localizations in the discs.
* **Expected yield.** n_exp = A_ref x (number of scored frames / number of T_ref frames), A_ref = N_ring of the same radial fit applied to T_ref (the same pores). It is the ring yield that the authors' own localizer gives on other frames; it is used only as the scale of the missed part below.
* **J_s** = N_ring / (N_ring + N_floor + N_miss), N_miss = max(0, n_exp - N_ring) (the structural analogue of the Jaccard index: ring localizations, floor localizations, missing ring yield).
* **E_s** = 100 - sqrt((100 - 100 J_s)^2 + (alpha w)^2), alpha = 1 per nm (the efficiency of the challenge, `smlm_common.efficiency`, with the radial width w in place of the RMSE).
* **w at equal yield** (the count control): w after thinning the larger of the two methods' ring localizations at random to the smaller one's N_ring (the fit is repeated; mean of 20 thinnings per bootstrap replicate). Reason: `real_frc.md` showed that a resolution measure improves with the number of localizations; the thinned width removes that.
* Secondary: mu - R_ref (radius bias); **corner contrast C8** (D2 only): the 8th azimuthal harmonic |sum_i exp(i 8 (theta_i - phi_j))| / N of the ring localizations (within +-2.5 w of mu) with the T_ref phases phi_j, against the null of phases permuted across pores (20 permutations), also at equal yield; ring localizations per pore per 1000 frames; the fraction of pores with at least 8 ring localizations; seconds per frame (CPU, one core, the machine's load is recorded); the fitted-width pile-up at the bounds and the chi2 of the ISS fit (as in `real_frc.md`).
* "As published" rows (D1: the authors' ThunderSTORM table; D2: the SMAP table of the same field), restricted to the scored frames and scored pores and scored with the same functions, with their authors' filters chosen on all frames. They are context, NOT comparators in the inference (T_ref comes from the same software, n_exp is calibrated on it, and its filters saw all frames). ThunderSTORM itself was not run (Java 21 is installed and the ImageJ hosts are reachable, but the plugin was not obtained).

## 5. Tuning on development data (same objective and search budget as `picasso_baseline.md` and `iss.md`)

* **Objective**: E_s of section 4 on the development frames and development pores (one objective, in place of the two, E and J, of the simulated study; J_s is reported next to it). Both methods are tuned for E_s with the same functions and the same development frames, and each tuned configuration is frozen before any test frame is localized. A constant position offset is NOT corrected (the pore centres come from another localizer, so an offset would be partly absorbed in c_j).
* **Picasso** (0.11.3, `picassosr==0.11.3`, single thread, through `smlm_common.run_picasso`): R0 = defaults (minimum net gradient 5000, box 7, `gausslq`, no padding); R1 = the 20-value net-gradient grid; R2 = the 108 structural settings (box 5/7/9, fit box 5/7/9, Gaussian filter off/1.0/1.5, padding 0/4, `gausslq`/`gaussmle`) with the coordinate-ascent search of the post-filters (net gradient, minimum photons, width window; at most 3 rounds), exactly the grids of `experiments/exp_smlm_picasso.py` (N1) with the values that carry a unit rescaled by one factor per dataset computed from the development frames alone (net gradient and photon limits by the ratio of the median net gradient / photons of the development frames' low-threshold Picasso run to the N1 reference value; the width windows by sigma0 / 140 nm), printed before the search. The number of scored configurations is reported (the simulated study scored 12,000 to 15,000 per set).
* **ISS** (`src/core/imaging.py` through `exp_smlm_iss`): 168 operating points = the 8 model configurations x 7 significance thresholds (4, 4.5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10) x 3 width bounds of `iss.md`, with the entry width, the width bounds and the resolution limit scaled by sigma0 / 1.4 px as in `real_frc.md`; no other degree of freedom (the elongated PSF of the camera is a declared model mismatch, as in `real_frc.md`). The label-free default ("a priori configuration" of `real_frc.md`, hp5h, kappa 5) is run on the test frames in addition and reported as a separate row (no tuning at all).
* The tuned configurations, the pore lists, the code commit and the scale factors are written to a file and hashed BEFORE the first test-frame localization; the file is committed.

## 6. Test run and statistics

* One test run per dataset, in which the frozen Picasso rows (R0, R1E, R2E; E means tuned for E_s) and the frozen ISS rows (tuned, label-free default) localize the test frames. A repeat is allowed only to fix a crash that leaves the numbers unchanged; every execution is listed.
* **Interval**: paired block bootstrap over the scored frames in blocks of 10 consecutive frames (an emitter stays on for several frames), 1000 resamples, seed 0, the same resampled blocks for both methods, the pores fixed (the reference is frozen), 95 % percentile intervals; the radial fit is redone in each replicate. A sensitivity interval resamples the pores (clusters, all their frames) instead (1000 resamples, seed 1). A claim must hold for both.
* **Primary outcomes** (two per dataset): (P1) Delta E_s = E_s(ISS tuned) - E_s(Picasso R2E tuned) on the test frames; (P2) Delta w at equal yield = w(ISS) - w(Picasso R2E), thinned as above (negative favours the ISS). Four intervals in all (2 datasets x 2 outcomes); each decision below uses all of them; no adjustment is applied because the rule is a conjunction. Everything else (J_s, mu bias, C8, yields, R0 / R1 rows, label-free ISS row, timing, "as published" rows, sensitivity intervals) is secondary and described, not tested.

## 7. What each outcome would mean

* **ISS better on real data** on a dataset: the 95 % interval of Delta E_s lies above 0 AND that of Delta w lies below 0 (both: more agreement with the geometry is not just more localizations). The paper may then say "on the real fields of view of D1 / D2 the ISS localizations agree better with the known pore geometry than those of a tuned Picasso", with the single-field and ring-model caveats of section 1. Only if this holds on BOTH D1 and D2 may it be written as a real-data advantage.
* **One of the two**: reported as mixed; the real-data sentence is limited to that dataset, with the other result stated next to it.
* **Equivalent**: both Delta E_s intervals within +-3 E points (the margin is arbitrary and fixed here) and no interval excluding 0: "no detectable difference on real data"; the abstract must not claim a real-data advantage.
* **DROP the real-data claim**: (i) Picasso better on at least one dataset (the upper limit of the Delta E_s interval below 0) and the ISS better on neither, or (ii) the ISS cannot be run on the data as specified (for example more than 12 CPU hours for the test frames after the declared reduction, or its development chi2 above 5 with the fitted widths at the bounds for more than half of the units: both are reported), or (iii) Picasso R2E's development E_s exceeds the ISS tuned E_s by more than 10 points on both datasets (the tuned ISS is not competitive before the test is even run; the test is still run once and reported). In every case the result is reported in full and the manuscript's real-data text is limited to the 2013 FRC experiment, which makes no claim of accuracy.
* **Void, not evidence**: validity gate V1 or V4 fails on a dataset (no valid ground truth there); gate V2 or V3 voids only the outcomes it names; a leak (any use of a test frame, or of a T_ref frame as a development or test frame) voids the run and is reported.
* A better ISS on D2 only because it returns more localizations (Delta E_s positive, Delta w at equal yield not negative) is NOT an advantage; a better precision at fewer localizations (Delta w negative, Delta E_s not positive) is reported as such.

## 8. Known weaknesses of the design, stated in advance

1. The geometry is partly derived from the data (R_ref, the pore centres and phases come from third-party tables of other frames of the same field). A localizer that shares systematic errors with that software (a bias in the pore radius, the same drift) is favoured; both Picasso and the ISS are independent of it.
2. D1's pores nearly touch, the ring model is a 1-D radial profile, the pooled floor is a model (uniform in area); a radial width includes the intrinsic ring width (antibody labelling) and the pore-to-pore radius spread, which is common to both methods.
3. Duplicated localizations of one emitter (splits) lie on the ring and raise N_ring and J_s; the equal-yield width does not punish them either. A frame-wise duplicate check (pairs within 1 sigma0 in one frame) is reported as a secondary count for each method; it is not part of the registered rule.
4. One camera per dataset, no calibration for D2: the ISS noise law is label-free; an error in it would affect the ISS only.
5. The ISS is evaluated in a window of 128 x 128 px and on at most 2000 (D1) / 1055 (D2) frames, Picasso on exactly the same; the time comparison (seconds per frame) is reported separately.
6. A 10 nm histogram and a 5 nm radial bin are used; the bin sizes are fixed now.

## 9. Order of events (the checklist this plan commits to)

1. Review and commit this plan (and, if the coordinator wishes, change what is open below). 2. Run the validity gates and the pore finder on T_ref only; write and hash the pore lists. 3. Label-free calibration of the ISS and Picasso scale factors on the development frames; time the ISS on 20 development frames; fix the frame budget. 4. Tune both methods on the development frames (E_s); freeze and hash the tuned configurations. 5. ONE test run per dataset. 6. Report all rows, intervals and the weaknesses above in `results/smlm/real_gt.md`, regenerated from a raw `.npz` (as in the earlier studies). D3 is analysed only after step 5, descriptively (pair-distance histogram of the localizations of both methods at their frozen D1 / D2 configurations, peak position and width; the pixel size is assumed 65 nm and unverified, so no inference on the spacing is made).

**Open for the coordinator's decision**: (a) whether D3 and the nano-mirror movie of D1 (a second field of view, 557 MB) should be added; (b) whether an origami grid dataset should be sought from the authors (Picasso origami samples; the Raab et al. raw movies) since none is public; (c) whether ThunderSTORM should be obtained and run (the plan only has its published table for D1); (d) the CPU budget for the ISS (this decides the frame numbers of section 2); (e) the margin of 3 E points and the 10-point development gate of section 7 are placeholders chosen without data.

## Amendments (coordinator's review; declared 2026-11-09 07:59 UTC, before any pore finder or localizer was run on any development or test frame)

**Amendment 1 (greedy-order row).** The controls of this repository (`results/astro/controls.md`, `results/smlm/controls.md`) showed that sliding matching pursuit with the same PSF, high-pass, weights, halo and stopping rule performs about as well as the dual-order ISS on simulated sets and on astronomy frames. ISS minus greedy is reported for P1 (Delta E_s) and P2 (Delta w at equal yield) as DESCRIPTIVE paired-bootstrap intervals (same blocks). All dual-order ISS runs use the same guard (`sliding_path_guarded`) as the controls.

**Amendment 2 (secondary rows).** ThunderSTORM (if it can be obtained and run headlessly within a few hours, tuned on the development frames for E_s over its own documented options with a budget it can reasonably have, and frozen) and the greedy row are SECONDARY: they do not change the decision rule of section 7, which stays as written (ISS tuned against Picasso R2E on P1 and P2, on D1 and D2).
