# Pre-registration: is there a regime in which the dual order of entry matters?

Workstream C of the improvement round.  Script: `experiments/exp_dualregime.py` (the engine, the scenes, the comparators and the report are all in it); report: `results/synthetic/dualregime.md`; raws: `dualregime_dev_raw.npz` (development) and `dualregime_raw.npz` (held-out).  This plan is committed BEFORE any held-out scene is scored.  Recorded (UTC): 2026-11-09T15:13:36Z (the commit that contains this file; no held-out scene had been generated or scored before it).

## 1. The question

The sliding inverse scale space (ISS, `order='dual'`: a candidate enters when its accumulated correlation reaches a common wall) and sliding matching pursuit (`order='greedy'`: the candidate of largest instantaneous correlation enters) differ only in the order of entry.  On SMLM frames and astronomy patches the controls (`results/smlm/controls.md`, `results/astro/controls.md`) found the greedy order within 0.016 F of the dual order and ahead on some sets, and the two close-pair experiments (`results/smlm/order_pairs*.md`) found the dual ahead for pairs at 1.2 to 2 widths under a BIC stop and behind it under a significance stop and at 3 widths.  Question: is there a regime, defined on development data, in which the dual order recovers the model order (the number and places of the features) better than (a) the greedy order with every other ingredient identical, (b) the greedy order followed by a free backward step, (c) the sliding Frank-Wolfe solver of the BLASSO (a fair classical comparator) by a margin larger than the Monte-Carlo error?  And under which conditions are the two orders equivalent?

## 2. What was seen before this plan (development data only)

* Exploratory scans with the same engine (scratch scripts, seeds 100000 to 200059): grid A (pairs: separation 1.2 to 5 widths, amplitude ratio 1 to 30, significance of the weaker 5 to 20; 96 cells of 60 scenes), part of grid B (random clusters of 4 and 7 features; 31 of 54 cells of 40 scenes), part of grid D (crowded windows; 13 of 24 cells of 50 scenes).  The final development scan (`--scan`, seeds from 1000000, all cells of A, B, C, D) is re-run with the committed code and is the development record of the report; it was started before this plan was written and its results are not used to change the plan except as stated in section 8.
* The existing post-hoc controls (order_pairs, controls) listed above.  A scratch version of the post-hoc SMLM analysis of section 7 was run before this plan; it is labelled POST HOC in the report whatever it shows.
* The final development scan of grid C (unequal widths free in [0.4, 3], 32 cells of 40 scenes, seeds from 1150000; the report's development section): over its 16 cells with four features the exact-recovery rate under BIC is 49 % (dual) against 56 % (greedy) and, after the backward step, 70 % against 66 %: the dual leads the greedy order by +4.8 points [+2.8, +7.0] with the step on both (pooled bootstrap over the 640 scenes of those cells), is behind the bare greedy order, and is far behind greedy + backward step (-16.6 [-20.3, -13.0]).  This is the prompt for regime W.
* What the exploration showed: (i) for separations of 2.5 widths and more the two orders return the same exact-recovery rate in almost all cells of grid A, with no scene in which the exactness of the two differs (paired difference 0 with standard error 0 in most cells); (ii) the only region with a systematic dual lead over the greedy order under BIC is separation 1.2 to 1.6 widths with weaker-feature significance 8 to 12 and amplitude ratio 1 to 3 (+3 to +15 points), and crowded windows of 3 features at mean spacing 1.6 to 3 widths (+4 to +20 points); (iii) a backward-elimination step on every block of the greedy path removes most of that lead; (iv) the sliding Frank-Wolfe comparator is ahead of the dual in the close-pair region (by 10 to 20 points at ratio 1 to 3); (v) the entrant-significance stop (`sig`) is a bad stop for the dual, so each order also gets its own natural rule as a sensitivity (`nat`).

## 3. Engine, scenes, comparators (fixed; all in the script)

* One-dimensional model of the whole pipeline: 96 samples at spacing 0.5, signal sum of non-negative Gaussians of width 1 (nominal), white Gaussian noise of known sd.  The significance of a feature is its amplitude times the kernel norm over the noise sd; the scene fixes the significance of the weakest feature (`snr`).  Dictionary: lattice of spacing 0.25 and width 1; resolution limit r_ex = 1.5 (as `imaging.sliding_path`, scaled to widths); block fit: non-negative least squares then trust-region fit of weights and centres (centres within 1.5 of their lattice starts; widths free in [0.4, 3] only in grid C); a unit with weight 0 and non-positive correlation leaves.  Dual and greedy run through the same `path` function, `order` is the only difference.  Paths run to K + 3 entries.
* Rules applied to the stored path (the same for every method): `bic` = the block of least chi^2 + 2 ln N k (a unit must lower chi^2 by 2 ln N = 9.1); `pen` = chi^2 + 16 k; `disc` = the discrepancy rule; `nat` = the dual's stopping time (kappa 4.5) against the greedy entrant significance (kappa 4.5); `oracleK` = the first block with exactly K units (no stopping rule: the order alone); `path` = some block of the path is exact.  `bicbw`, `penbw`: the same rules after backward elimination of every block of at most K + 1 units (remove the unit whose removal raises chi^2 least while that is below the penalty, others refitted).
* Comparators: the greedy order (`greedy`), the greedy order with the backward step (`greedy` under `bicbw`), and sliding Frank-Wolfe (`baselines.sfw`, `exp_noise_order`'s ladder of eight penalties lam = f max|<y phi>|, f from 0.5 to 0.003, fixed a priori; spikes of own significance below 3 dropped) with the penalty chosen by the same BIC / penalty-16 rule as the other methods: the same tuning budget (no constant is tuned for any method on the held-out data; the single constants are fixed here).
* Exact recovery: K-hat = K and a one-to-one match within 0.7 widths of the true centres.  Position rms of the exact ones.

## 4. Held-out data (never used in any scan; seeds from 7000000 in steps of 10000 per cell; 2-D from 9000000)

* Regime P (close pairs): separation d in {1.2, 1.6} widths, amplitude ratio in {1, 3}, significance of the weaker in {8, 12}: 8 cells of 250 scenes (2000).
* Regime Q (crowded windows): the whole grid D (K in {3, 5} features in a window of K gap widths with gap in {1.6, 2.2, 3.0}, minimum separation 1, amplitude ratio in {1, 6}, significance of the weakest in {8, 14}): 24 cells of 100 scenes (2400), fresh seeds.
* Regime W (unequal widths): the K = 4 cells of grid C (clusters of four features with minimum separation in {1.5, 2.5}, amplitude ratio in {1, 5}, significance of the weakest in {6, 12}, widths log-uniform in [1/w, w] with w in {1.5, 2}, the dictionary's widths free in [0.4, 3]): 16 cells of 120 scenes (1920).  Chosen after the development scan of grid C, in which the dual leads the greedy order only after the backward step (see section 2); the sliding Frank-Wolfe comparator has a fixed width and is shown but not counted in this regime.
* Regime E (equivalence): separation in {3.5, 5.0}, ratio in {1, 10}, significance in {8, 12}: 8 cells of 100 scenes (800).
* Part T (noise-free, deterministic): pairs at separations 1 to 6 widths, ratios 1 to 20, resolution limit 0 and 1.5; 12 lattice phases each.
* Two-dimensional arm: the scene generator and the two pipelines of `exp_order_pairs` (EM-CCD frames, known Gaussian PSF, `fit_frame` with `order='dual'` and `'greedy'`, stop BIC 4.0, nothing else changed), equal fluxes, separations {1.2, 1.6, 2.5, 3.0} widths, photons {2000, 6000}: 8 cells of 100 scenes with seeds from 9000000.  The run order is T, P, E, Q, W, two-dimensional arm; the report is generated once all parts exist.

## 5. The single primary metric and the rules that decide the verdict

Metric: exact model-order recovery under the `bic` rule (or `bicbw` where the arm says so), paired over scenes (the same data for every method).  For each comparison: the mean paired difference in points with a 95 % percentile bootstrap interval over scenes (2000 resamples, seed 0), pooled over the cells of the regime (equal weights: the cells have equal numbers of scenes).  Win = interval above 0, loss = below 0, tie = contains 0.

Comparisons (ids prefixed by the regime letter): 1 dual+bw vs greedy+bw (the order alone, both with the backward step); 2 dual vs greedy (as the pipelines stand); 3 dual vs greedy+bw; 4 dual vs sFW; sensitivity 5 (dual+bw vs sFW), 6 to 8 (the same under penalty 16), 9 (`oracleK`: order alone, no stopping rule), 10 (`nat`: each order's own rule).  Localisation: the paired position rms where both are exact.

**Rule for the claim "the dual order matters in regime R" (R = P or Q): comparisons 1, 3 and 4 must all be wins; in regime W (a width-free regime, where the fixed-width Frank-Wolfe comparator is misspecified) comparisons 1 and 3.**  If any is a tie or loss the claim is not made; the report says which failed.  Comparison 2 alone is the claim that the dual beats the bare greedy order, which the paper may state only with the qualifier that a backward step or a convex solver removes it, if 1 or 3 or 4 fail.
**Rule for "equivalent" in E:** the BIC blocks of the two orders are the same set (sorted centres within 0.05) in at least 98 % of the scenes, judged by the lower end of the 95 % Clopper-Pearson interval over the 800 pooled scenes.
**Two-dimensional arm:** the resolved fraction (exactly two units, each within 0.7 widths of one emitter, one-to-one) of the dual minus that of the greedy order, pooled over the separations {1.2, 1.6} ("close") and {2.5, 3.0} ("mid") separately, the same bootstrap; win/tie/loss as above.

## 6. Stated expectations (written before the run, so that the verdict is not read into the data afterwards)

P: 2 win, 1 tie, 3 tie, 4 loss (so: the claim is not made).  Q: 2 win; 1 win or tie (not predicted); 4 loss (so: not made).  W: 1 win (about +3 to +5 points), 2 loss, 3 loss (so: the claim is not made, but the order-only comparison 1 is a small genuine dual advantage).  E: equivalent.  Part T (exploratory values: 12 phases per cell): the entry sequences agree in at least 11 of 12 phases at separation 5 widths and above, and in at most 2 of 12 at separation 3.5 widths and below; the final two-entry block is exact in the greedy order in every phase, and in the dual order in every phase except at 2.5 to 3.5 widths when r_ex = 0 (9 to 11 of 12).  Two-dimensional: close = dual win, mid = dual loss.

## 7. POST HOC (not tests, not pre-registered)

(a) The SMLM test sets: from the stored test detections of the dual ISS and the greedy order (at the dual's E-tuned point) in `results/smlm/controls_raw.npz`, recall of ground-truth emitters by the distance to their nearest neighbour in the frame (bands 0-200, 200-300, 300-450, 450+ nm; the PSF sd is about 150 nm), block bootstrap over frames.  (b) The astronomy patches: not re-run (the stored raws hold only aggregates per patch; a re-run of the greedy order costs 2 to 6 minutes per patch and the truth catalogue is not stored with a per-star match); nothing is claimed about faint stars next to bright ones in the astronomy data.

## 8. What will be dropped, what will be reported whatever the outcome

Nothing is dropped after the held-out scenes are seen: every cell is reported; a scene that raises an exception is counted as a failure to recover (for all methods the same scene is lost and the run is repeated unchanged once; the count is printed).  No constant of any method is changed after this plan is committed; if a bug is found in the engine, the held-out run is repeated in full with a new seed block and both runs are reported.  The development scan (all cells of A, B, C, D) is reported as exploratory: with about 200 cells a few cells will show a difference of two standard errors by chance (the report counts them).  If the final development scan contradicts section 2 materially (for example a regime in grid B, C or D with a dual lead surviving the backward step and the sFW comparator), the plan would be amended by a dated addendum BEFORE the held-out run, and the amendment would name the cells that prompted it.

## 9. Wording the report supports in each outcome (decided now)

* If the claim is made in some regime: "in regime R (…) the dual order recovers the model order better than the greedy order, the greedy order with a backward step and the sliding Frank-Wolfe comparator, by x points [interval] on held-out synthetic data; on SMLM/astronomy data the regime is (not) present".
* If it is not made (expected): "the dual order of entry is not the source of the method's gains: with every other ingredient identical it is equivalent to the greedy order for separations of about 3.5 widths and more (and the entry sequences are identical from about 5 widths, the continuum entry threshold), and in the sub-resolution regime where it leads the bare greedy order the lead is removed by a backward step or exceeded by the sliding Frank-Wolfe solver of the BLASSO; the dual order is an analysis tool (it makes the path computable from the training dynamics and the entry times a statistic), not an accuracy mechanism."
