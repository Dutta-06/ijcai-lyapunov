## Conclusions (generated; verdict words from the tables)

1. **Equivalence.**  For separations of 3.5 widths and more (regime E) the two orders select the same block in at least 98 % of the scenes (plan rule met), and in part T the noise-free entry sequences coincide from about 5 widths, the order of the continuum entry threshold of the Gram (4.7 to 5.8 widths).  This is what the structure of the two orders predicts: when the Gram matrix of the features is nearly diagonal, the instantaneous correlation of every feature not yet explained is constant in time (fitting the others does not change it), so the accumulated dual of a feature is its constant correlation times the elapsed time, and both orders sort the same numbers, the amplitudes.  They can differ only through the coherence between features.  Scope: this is for one-dimensional pairs with two features (amplitude ratios 1 and 10, separations 3.5 and 5 widths) (797 of 800 scenes returned the same block) and for the FINAL BIC blocks, not for the entry sequences (in the noise-free part T they differ at 3.5 widths and less, see the summary) and not for images: in the two-dimensional arm the dual order against the greedy order is a loss at the mid separations (2.5, 3 widths), and two dimensions at 3.5 widths and more were not run.
2a. **Regime P (close pairs).**  As the pipelines stand the dual order against the bare greedy order is a win (P2); with the same backward step on both, the order alone is a win (P1); against greedy + backward step a win (P3); with no stopping rule at all (the first block with K units) a win (P9).  This is the order effect, everything else equal, (P1) and (P2); the comparison with the sliding Frank-Wolfe solver of the BLASSO is a comparison with a different algorithm, not an order comparison, and the dual is a loss there (P4).  The oracle-K comparison (P9) uses the true number of units.  The plan rule for the claim that the dual order matters in P is NOT met.
2b. **Regime Q (crowded windows).**  As the pipelines stand the dual order against the bare greedy order is a win (Q2); with the same backward step on both, the order alone is a win (Q1); against greedy + backward step a win (Q3); with no stopping rule at all (the first block with K units) a win (Q9).  This is the order effect, everything else equal, (Q1) and (Q2); the comparison with the sliding Frank-Wolfe solver of the BLASSO is a comparison with a different algorithm, not an order comparison, and the dual is a loss there (Q4).  The oracle-K comparison (Q9) uses the true number of units.  The plan rule for the claim that the dual order matters in Q is NOT met.
3. **Regime W (four features of unequal widths, widths free).**  Dual against greedy as the pipelines stand: loss (W2); with the same backward step: win (W1); dual against greedy + backward step: loss (W3); first block with K units: loss (W9).  The plan rule (W1 and W3) is NOT met.
4. **Two dimensions (fresh scenes of the SMLM close-pair experiment).**  Close separations: dual against greedy a win; mid separations: a loss.
5. **Mechanism (a hypothesis, not a finding).**  The data are CONSISTENT WITH early correlation memory being what lets the dual order lead where it does; this was not tested: there is no ablation (a leaky or reset accumulator was not run), and the evidence is one planted scene and an inspection of the first merged two-dimensional scenes (not scripted).  The picture: when two features are close the first atom is fitted to their union and absorbs part of the second; the residual correlation at the second feature is then no larger than the noise maxima elsewhere in the window, and matching pursuit would enter a noise peak first (the planted scene of the tests: seed 100002 of the pair at 1.6 widths).  The accumulated dual would still carry the early correlation, which both features contributed to, and so enter the second feature first.  A backward step (removing a unit whose removal costs less than the penalty) removes most of the noise units that matching pursuit then carries, which is consistent with the lead shrinking from the pipelines-as-they-stand comparison to the same-backward-step comparison.  The same memory could be a liability when a unit is fitted exactly: the duals of its neighbours have accumulated to nearly the wall and, without a resolution limit, the dual enters ghosts next to it (the planted wide-Gaussian test shows the ghosts; the resolution limit of the pipelines suppresses them).  A second possible way the memory hurts, suggested by the two-dimensional arm at 2.5 to 3 widths (an inspection of the first merged scenes at 3 widths, hence a hypothesis), is that the first atom, entered at the midpoint of a resolved pair, slides towards one emitter while the accumulated (possibly stale) dual of the other emitter's lattice points keeps its head start: the dual would enter the already-covered emitter next and leave the first atom as a weak third unit (a split), where matching pursuit, which sees the residual after the slide, enters the uncovered emitter.  The post hoc SMLM table below is the only check on real frames available here and is not a confirmation.
6. **What the paper may claim.**  The dual order of entry is not what makes the method better than the classical solvers: in the regimes where it leads the bare greedy order, the lead is modest once a backward step is allowed and it is exceeded by the sliding Frank-Wolfe solver of the BLASSO (a different algorithm: that is not an order comparison).  The backward step reduces the lead over the bare greedy order but does not remove it: from +7.8 (P2) and +8.0 (Q2) points to +2.1 (P1) and +3.4 (Q1) points, that is to about 2-3 points (P1 win, Q1 win).  The accumulated-dual order is the object that the small-initialisation training dynamics computes and that the theory analyses (entry times as a statistic, the stopping time); it is an analysis tool and a modest advantage in crowded, near-resolution scenes, not a source of accuracy over the best classical comparator.
7. **Where the plan's stated expectations were not met.**  The comparisons P1 and P3 were predicted ties and are small wins; in part T a few cells fall short of the counts the plan quoted for 5 widths and above (and one cell at 3.5 widths and below exceeds them), the pooled counts being in the summary; the plan's other stated expectations were met.
8. **Design caveats (the referee's review; not corrected in the stored runs).**  The backward step is applied only to blocks of at most K_true + 1 units (`apply_rules` uses `len(c_true)`), so the true number of features leaks into the backward-step arms (comparisons 1, 3, 5, 7).  The referee's rerun without the cap on held-out scenes (180 P, 120 W, 80 Q; not recomputed here) left P identical and moved greedy + backward step from 78 to 82 of 120 in W (dual + backward step from 81 to 82) and from 8 to 10 of 80 in Q: P1, Q1 and W1 are inflated by about 0 to 3 points.  The comparisons marked oracle K use the true number of units by construction, and the comparisons against the sliding Frank-Wolfe solver and the comparisons with each method's own stopping rule are not order comparisons (see the status column).
