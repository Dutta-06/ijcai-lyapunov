## D. Depth 2's convergence in ε

D2 (w = u² − v², s² = ε) on the design (target 0) and the first targets of C's sample (all of family A, the easy family). Its wall is at η = ln(1/ε)/(4 lr). The formed threshold θ is crossed before the wall (at |w| ≈ 1), so the **first entrant** is early by the factor ln(1/θ)/ln(1/ε) to leading order: its *τ err.* ≈ ln(1/θ)/ln(1/ε), and *τ err. × ln(1/ε)* is a constant set by θ (ln(1/0.009) = 4.71 for the design's θ), not a fitted law. A later entrant is early by the same factor only while its dual is straight until its wall (separated features, as on these targets, whose median follows the law); where a dual bends before its wall the earliness is larger and the entry times are not the path's up to one common factor (Part C, D2 at ε = 1e-06 on 200 targets: median τ err. 0.339, 95th percentile 0.654); Part K's coupled targets show the mechanism for the pairs. What decides the outcome is the margin μ (below).

## K. Branch pairs: the paper's logarithmic regime

Paper 17's Proposition (starts on branches): a kernel started at α(0) ≠ 0 sits on a branch of its curve, its mass growing as ε·exp(γ a(0)(η − η(0))), with the weight ρ = 1/(γ\|a(0)\|) and the logarithmic scale L_ε = ln(1/ε), τ = t/L_ε; two kernels of opposite signs on the same atom give the two-sided box, the limit being the path of equal weights. **That proposition is proved for fixed centres. Its sliding extension (the centres move, the limit is the sliding path of the atoms), which is what is run here, is tested, not proved:** Paper 17's theorem on the sliding limit is proved for starts with α(0) = 0. Part G's random α_i(0) do not test it (their weights differ by decades); here every one of the 21 centres carries a *pair*, α(0) = +a₀ and −a₀, masses ε (42 units, `PaperPairs`), τ = t/scale with scale = ln(1/ε)/(γ a_max tanh(a₀/a_max)) = L_ε ρ, the time at which the path of unit weights has τ = 1, against the sliding path of the 21-atom dictionary. *formed*: units above the formed threshold (in brackets, the atoms that have at least one such unit); *set*, *order*, *τ rel. err.* are the atoms' (an atom forms when its first unit does); *exact*: K atoms formed, one unit each, every feature within 0.05σ and 5% of a unit; *τ ratio err.*: the entry times up to a common factor (t_j/t_first against the path's τ_j/τ_first); *gap K*: log₁₀ of the K-th over the (K+1)-th largest \|w\| over all units (threshold-free). *predicted*: the factor 1 − c/L_ε by which the **first entrant** of the path is early, derived from the branch law with no fitted parameter (`PaperPairs.form_ratio`: along the curve dη = dβ/(γaβ), so a kernel has \|w\| = θ after the mirror coordinate has grown by (1/γ)∫dβ/(aβ) from β = ε, against the wall's L_ε/(γ\|a(0)\|); a dual that is a straight line to its wall reaches the formed level at the fraction 1 − c/L_ε of its entry time; c is the constant of the column τ rel. err. × ln(1/ε)). A later atom is early by the same factor only while its dual is straight until its wall (separated features); an earlier entry changes its dual's slope and the formed level 1 − c/L_ε is then crossed on another part of the dual, so its earliness differs at the same order 1/L_ε. The tables on the design and on the 10 targets of family A below (features at least 2.3σ apart) are where it looks exact; the coupled targets at the end show where it fails. The common factor is therefore claimed for the first entrant, and for a later atom only while its dual is straight. ε = 1e-12 is pre-asymptotic for the pairs: L_ε = 27.6 and the early factor is 0.225 (a₀ = 0.1), 0.147 (a₀ = 0.3) on the design, against 0.0281 and 0.0183 at ε = 1e-96 (L_ε = 221); the pairs are therefore run down to ε = 1e-96, with D2 as the control there (P0 and D3 run on the power scale ε^(−1/2), which this engine does not integrate in a practical time at those ε; they are run at ε = 1e-06, 1e-12 only). Controls: the same targets with P0 started at α(0) = 0, D3 and D2.

## L. The dictionary's spacing

Dictionaries of n kernels of width 0.12 at spacing 2/(n − 1) on [−1, 1] (61 samples), each readout against the sliding path of its own dictionary. *off-grid*: the design's features (the part B target); *on-grid*: the features moved onto the nearest kernel centres, at every spacing (neighbouring kernels overlap by exp(−spacing²/(4σ²)), which is above 0.5 below spacing 0.2). The on-grid arm is a control that removes the sliding — the dictionary contains the features, so no unit has to move onto one — and not another spacing sweep of the same problem; D2 passes there iff the same margin rule holds. The path's runner-up margins (`margins`): μ, the smallest 1 − \|u_j\| of a kernel not yet active at an entry (the later entrants included), and μ never, the same over the kernels that never enter (NaN when every kernel enters: read as +∞, nothing is left to violate the rule). *rule* (D2 rows): depth 2 forms the path's set iff the margin exceeds ln(1/θ)/ln(1/ε) (Part D; the threshold is printed, then the rule's prediction of *set = path* from μ never); *gap K*: log₁₀ of the K-th over the (K+1)-th largest \|w\|; *pos err*, *amp err*: the largest distance of a feature from its unit (σ) and the largest relative amplitude error, when K units have formed.
