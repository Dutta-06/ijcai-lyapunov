# Exact recovery of separated features: the path against its decoupled limit

Generated by `experiments/exp_separated_regime.py` from `separated_regime_raw.npz`; every number below is printed by the script (protocol: its docstring). 1-D, sigma = 0.12, 21 starting kernels of spacing h = 0.1, equal masses, `sliding_iss_nd`; 60 targets per row. Gamma(d) = exp(-d^2 / (4 sigma^2)) is the overlap of two kernels at the distance d; Delta is the separation of the features (for K = 3 the target's actual minimal separation; the row's Delta is the lower bound it was drawn with). "Decoupled" is the prediction by K one-feature paths running in parallel (the best starting kernel of each feature, entered at T_k = 1 / |a_j|). Exact: final rms residual below 1e-06, exactly K units, no exit. "Gate": min(mu0, gap) > Gamma(Delta - h/2), where mu0 is the smallest margin of a feature's best kernel over its runner-up and gap the smallest relative gap between two features' hitting times of the decoupled limit: an empirical, lattice-specific law, not the theorem's condition (the theorem's constants put its Delta_0 above these separations, referee round IS2). The entry-time error is the largest over the entries of |tau_k - T_k| / T_k, taken over the targets whose entries ARE the decoupled prediction's (same kernels, same order); it is divided by Gamma(Delta - h/2) of the same target.
